# Judge: chapter 3, The Ninth Wall
**Overall: 7/10**

| Art | Hands and arms | Storytelling | Lettering | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7/10 | 6.5/10 | 7.5/10 | 6.5/10 | 7/10 |

This is a good chapter, and a reader will finish it wanting chapter 4. The nine-panel flashback earns Nib's refusal. "Nobody came back for me" becomes "Everyone comes back. That's the rule." and then "You called me back." The trap is set up fairly and pays off with weight. The cooldown windows (01:00, 00:12, 00:03, 00:00) give the fight a ticking clock, and the last four panels turn the boss into someone Nib knew. The art is polished, the character designs hold in almost every panel, and most hands hold what they grip. What keeps it below Originals level is a cluster of slips at the climax, where readers look hardest:
- P45 loses the trap's geography.
- P58 leaves the knight's sword floating with no hand on it.
- The seal-break window, which carries the chapter's power-up, is too small to read on a phone.
- The narration twice describes something its panel doesn't show (P13, P55).

None of these needs a rethink. Fix them and this is a 7.5 to 8 chapter.

## Strengths
- **A complete emotional arc built on one promise.** The line runs "THEN WE COME BACK FOR YOU" (P01) → "NOBODY CAME BACK FOR ME." (P09) → "EVERYONE COMES BACK. THAT'S THE RULE." (P16) → "YOU CALLED ME BACK." (P50) → "I TOLD YOU. EVERYONE COMES BACK." (P51). "GO FIRST, KETT. I'LL BE RIGHT BEHIND YOU." (P03) quietly echoes the series title. The grey flashback, with Kett's yellow scarf as its only colour, is the best-designed sequence in the chapter.
- **A trap the reader can follow without the narrator.** First the clue (P20: bordered plates and spike holes), then the notebook sketch (P21) and the overhead plan (P22), then CLICK / SHHNNNK / KA-DOOOM (P31–P33). The big pages (P06, P30, P33, P40, P47, P53) have real scale and impact. The dark "voice" balloons in P59, P62 and P63 make the cliffhanger land.
- **Design discipline.** The fixed details stay correct in nearly every panel I checked, back views and reversed angles included:
  - Jaeha: pencil behind his right ear, fringe over his left eye, both T patches, gloves.
  - Nib: mask on his left eye, notched left ear, left fang, two knives, bare feet.
  - The knight: cracked left pauldron, nine-tower sigil (I counted 9 in P10, P30, P59 and P63).

## Problems, most damaging first
1. **[page_30, P45]** The trap's geography breaks at its height. The knight kneels in front of the lowered portcullis rather than under it. Jaeha stands on the same side, a few metres away, with no bars between them. P33, P34 and P44 showed the gate on the knight's back and the bars between it and Jaeha, so here the reader can't tell what is holding the boss. Fix: redraw with the portcullis across the knight's back and Jaeha on the far side, his hand on a bar.
2. **[page_36, P52]** The chapter's payoff window can't be read on a phone. Measured on the 1080-px strip:
   - "SEAL 1/5 → 2/5 · POWER 20% → 40%": capitals about 10 px tall.
   - The header: about 15 px.
   - Balloon lettering, for comparison: about 26 px.
   - **[page_11, P14]** has the same problem: its body lines are 12–13 px.

   Fix: set window body text at balloon size and cut words ("SEAL 2/5" / "POWER 20% → 40%"). Drop "OPERATOR INTERFACE" if it doesn't fit.
3. **[page_40, P58]** The knight's sword hilt hangs in mid-air beside its helm with no gauntlet on the grip, while the blade still rests on Nib's knives. It reads as a floating sword in the panel that sets up the reveal. Fix: put the knight's right gauntlet on the grip, as in P62.
4. **[page_11, P13]** The narration contradicts the art. "The amber glow drains out of Nib's eye" is read over a vivid amber iris with a tear; the dull eye only appears in P15 and P17. Fix: grey the iris here, add a second beat where it fades, or change the line to "...and the amber begins to fade."
5. **[page_15, P21]** The whole plan is spelled out before it happens ("SPIKES FROM BOTH PILLARS, THEN THE PORTCULLIS"), and P22 diagrams it. P31–P33 then play exactly as announced, so the trap has no surprise beat. Fix: hold one card back. Drop "THEN THE PORTCULLIS" from the note and the gate from the diagram, so the slam in P33 is a reveal.
6. **[page_18, P26]** The SNAP insert is a close-up whose only subject is the hand, and the hand reads wrong: two digits on the cover, three under, and the thumb's root hidden. Fix: put the thumb on the cover and four fingers under the back board.
7. **[page_02, P02]** The tail of "YES, COMMANDER." ends on the round tower's roof, about 300 px above Nib, so the tower seems to answer. Fix: run the tail down to Nib.
8. **[page_36, P52]** The reveal comes early. Nib already wears the blue-lined cloak and the bronze tower medallion here, so P53's narration ("the Ninth Wall's colours flood back into his cloak, with the nine-tower sigil at his throat") describes a change the reader has already seen. Fix: keep him in the plain charcoal cloak, glow only, until the splash.
9. **[page_36, P51]** The balloon tail runs straight across Jaeha's raised hand in the emotional payoff panel. Fix: bring the tail in above the fingers to his mouth, or move the balloon up and left.
10. **[page_38, P55]** The narration says "He looks back over his shoulder", but Nib is drawn facing Jaeha square-on while holding a sword off behind his own head. Both the line and the block read awkwardly. Fix: keep the P54 stance with only his head turned back, or change the line to match the drawing.
11. **[page_43, P63]** Continuity: one panel after Nib holds the sword off with crossed knives (P62), his arms are flung wide and the knives and blade are gone. Fix: keep the crossed knives and the blade in the top of the frame, or add a beat where the knight lifts the sword away.
12. **[page_05, P05]** The narration sometimes repeats the page instead of adding to it:
    - Here, "Far away, the sound every soldier on that wall was waiting for" restates the caption's "FROM A FORTRESS FAR AWAY".
    - "The notebook snaps shut" (page_18, P26) restates the SNAP.
    - "A hand's width" is used twice (P12, P41).

    Fix: trim or replace these lines so the narrator adds something each time.

## Hands and arms
- **[page_40, P58]** The knight has no hand on its sword. The hilt floats beside the helm with no gauntlet on the grip (one arm hangs at the right edge), yet the blade still presses on Nib's knives.
- **[page_18, P26]** Jaeha's hand snapping the notebook shut. Two digits lie on the cover (the lower one with a thumb-sized nail) and three fingertips curl underneath; the thumb's root is hidden in the glove. It reads as the book wedged between the index and middle fingers.
- **[page_38, P55]** Nib, both arms. They are raised overhead with the wrists bent back, holding the crossed knives against a blade that comes from behind him while his body faces the other way. Possible, but strained and hard to read.
- **[page_43, P63]** Nib, both arms. They are flung up in a wide V with the hands off-panel, straight after the crossed-knife block in P62, with no beat between; the knives vanish.
- **[page_17, P24]** Nib's hands, clasped round his knees: the two hands' fingers interleave confusingly (minor).
- **[page_34, P49]** Yerin's hands on her knees: the fingers merge where the two hands meet (minor).
- **[page_36, P51]** Jaeha's raised right hand: the balloon tail cuts straight across the fingers. That is a lettering fault, but it spoils the hand.
- Checked at full size and sound:
  - the commander's fist and pointing hand (P02);
  - the four hands on the message tube (P03);
  - Nib's knife grips (P06, P15, P28, P49, P50, P53, P54, P62);
  - the tally-carving grip (P07);
  - Jaeha's hands reaching, holding the notebook and pencil, shielding, giving the thumbs-up and on the floor (P10, P16, P21, P33, P37, P44, P48);
  - the knight's gauntlets (P30, P33, P40, P42, P47).

## To gain one point
1. **Repair the climax's continuity.**
   - Redraw P45 with the gate on the knight's back and Jaeha behind the bars.
   - Put a gauntlet on the sword in P58.
   - Carry the crossed-knife block through to P63.

   These are the panels readers study hardest, and at the moment the art contradicts the action in them.
2. **Make the two system windows the story depends on (P52, P14) readable at phone size.** Use balloon-size body text and fewer words.
3. **Hold one card back in the plan, and fix the two narration lines that contradict their panels.** Drop the portcullis from P21 and P22 so the trap has a surprise. Bring the narration for P13 and P55 into line with the drawings.
