## 1. The series in three lines

- **Logline.** In a Seoul where gates open in grocery aisles, summoned constructs haul crates for less than minimum wage, and draw tickets are sold like lottery scratch cards, a broke night-shift clerk spends his one free draw "just to see". He summons a *person*.
- **Promise.** A man who will not go into dungeons sends extraordinary companions where he cannot go. He learns to carry their weight, literally, from the outside, and builds the strongest guild in the world on two rules: **anyone can leave, and everyone comes back.**
- **Question.** In a world that sends its poorest people in first, can someone who needs others to take the danger build something that isn't ownership?

## 3. What stays from the published chapters and Abafadot's v3, and what changes

**Kept (canon):**
- Taeo: cautious orphan, grocery clerk, supports his family, low output, refuses to enter dungeons.
- Nib: adult goblin scout, talks, remembers a watch post, wants to go home, not a mascot or a hidden god.
- Ordinary summons are nonverbal constructs.
- Gacha-style roster growth is slow.
- Every summon is a person who can say no.
- Fusion appears later, in Part Two, and is irreversible.
- The guild/empire grows to "unmatched" around ch 200.
- The power ladder: phase-linked force transfer, plus reservoir, relay and lattice.
- The boss-clear return-mark rule.
- The six S-ranks.
- Chae, Minho, Han Seoryun.
- The money details: ₩24,800, ₩20,000 for an hour, ₩7,000 noodles.
- The cup of water, "I'M NOT THAT ONE", "DOES HE EAT FISH?"

**Changed, and why:**
1. **Stakes from chapter 2.** The digest found zero stakes for seven chapters and only one real end-hook. Now:
   - Nib's legal status is on a clock (an unregistered-arrival notice, then a classification review in which a research institute petitions to file him as *property*).
   - Taeo's job is being automated by constructs.
   - A ₩50,000,000 offer for Nib lands on a man with ₩24,800.
2. **Taeo gets an inner voice.** Sparse floating-text narration, ART_BIBLE B3. His fear, humour and want are on the page.
3. **Pace.** The published seven chapters (about four days) are retold in **chapters 1–7 with more happening**. The family visit moves to ch 8. The first crossing comes at ch 10–11, not "later".
4. **The supporting cast is named and wants things:**
   - Eunchae (coworker): a food-licence exam and a stolen deposit.
   - Haru (brother).
   - Grandma Okja.
   - Grandpa Gilsu.
   - Bae Jungho (courier): he is in debt for his golem.
   - Ryu Minho: inherits his family guild's debt.
   - Im Sejin (medic).
   - Ryu Haein (Minho's engineer sister, new).
   - Gu Dami (teen porter, new).
5. **A hidden history (new, the "Void Century").**
   - Thirty-one years ago, the **Long Night**, the first wave of gates, pushed a few living people through from the other side.
   - The state filed them as "anomalous entities". A research institute (**Kanghan**) studied them in a facility called **the Annex**, and the records were sealed.
   - A handful of these **Old Arrivals** still live in Seoul without papers.
   - The "arrival report" Chae files for Nib is a leftover of that era's law.
   - This fits v3's source-network answer: an emergency network that moved populations. It makes the institute's interest in Nib sinister without making anyone a cartoon. Part One plants it; Part Two (ch 101–140) opens it.
6. **Taeo's parents get a cause** (v3 left it undecided).
   - Nine years ago his father, a contracted **porter**, and his mother, a **camp cook**, died in the **Seogang camp collapse**. It was a gate-site accident on a job subcontracted three times down to the cheapest bidder, and rescue was held up while response contracts were priced.
   - In the same month, Han Seoryun's broadcast showed her **going back for the porters** in a different gate.
   - She is NOT responsible for his loss. She is the opposite of what happened to his parents. This is why he idolises her and why he will not go in.
7. **The power gets a heart.**
   - Taeo's force-transfer faculty means he literally **takes part of the impact his companions suffer into his own body**, from outside the gate.
   - The interface calls it **[LOAD SHARE]**. He calls it "carrying".
   - It has limits and recoil, and it hurts. This turns "the coward who stays outside" into "the one who carries the weight", and his courage becomes visible without a sword.
8. **The draw economy is a real-world mirror.**
   - Draw tickets are a consumer product: SumEx, ₩99,000 a ticket, 0.3% "rare".
   - Draw loans (Bluebird Capital) fund people who dream of leasing a rare construct to logistics platforms. Most pull commons and owe for years.
   - Taeo's interface is different in kind: it reaches *people*, and from ch 21 it only **invites**.
9. **The guild's name is a reclaimed insult.**
   - At the exhibition a streamer's mocking clip, "HIS SUMMONS GO FIRST!", goes viral (ch 51), and the crowd chants it at the final (ch 57).
   - Taeo answers on live mic (ch 61). The phrase becomes the slogan, and the guild founded at ch 100 is called **GO FIRST**.
   - Its emblem is an open door with one footprint stepping out, plus three dots (the knock).

**Rules for issues.**
1. Every issue arrives as a specific person's problem with a price in won.
2. Nobody delivers a speech about "society". People argue from their own interests.
3. Every antagonist believes they are being reasonable, and some of them are partly right: Kanghan cares about science, Baek's consolidation really does fund rescue gear, and Dokgo is himself squeezed by the contractor above him.
4. The story can be funny about all of it.

## 5. The world (rules that bind every script)

### Gates and dungeons
- **The Long Night.** Gates have opened for 31 years, since the first night when 400 opened at once.
- **What a gate is.** A gate is a door-height black-violet slit with a cold rim. Inside is a dungeon whose sky is the wrong colour.
- **Grades.** A gate's grade (E to S) predicts its danger.
- **Closing.** Killing the dungeon's **boss** closes that gate (a local connection only; v3 F17).
- **Return marks (shown on page in ch 10, used from then on).**
  - Every living entrant gets a one-use outward **return mark** when crossing.
  - When the boss dies, entry closes, and a conscious bearer can trigger their mark at once.
  - After 30 seconds the mark returns its bearer automatically, unconscious or not, with worn and carried gear.
  - Marks do not heal or revive anyone.
  - A native of the dungeon who never crossed has no mark.
- **Anchor fragments.**
  - Boss territories shed a few finite **anchor fragments** into the ground around them. Teams collect them during the approach, while the boss lives.
  - They power hospital devices, stabilisers and industry. This is the gate economy.

### People with abilities
- About 1 in 600 people "awaken".
- **Hunters** are ranked E to S by the Ability Assessment Office (AAO).
- **Guilds** employ hunters. **Survey crews** map gates before a clear. **Porters** carry, for day rates, often unawakened.
- **Summoners** instantiate **constructs** (golems, mannequins, beasts): nonverbal patterns that follow simple commands.
  - In law, constructs are registered equipment.
  - Elite summoners such as Park Doyun run sophisticated constructs through practised attention.

### The draw economy
- **SumEx** (the Summoning Exchange) sells **draw tickets** at ₩99,000 each. A ticket buys one instantiation by a licensed summoner.
  - Most draws produce common porters.
  - The 0.3% "rares" lease to logistics platforms for big monthly fees.
- **Bluebird Capital** lends against future draws: the **draw loan**.
- **Constructs work for platforms** (QuickHaul couriers, warehouse chains, Hanbit Mart's head office). Human night-shift and porter jobs shrink.

### Taeo's operator interface (what only he has)
- **What it is.** An old **emergency protocol** from the other side. It is slightly damaged, and his activation was a damaged automatic selection, not destiny (v3).
- **How it looks.** It speaks in floating windows only he sees (ART_BIBLE B4). Others see nothing; sometimes it glitches.
- **Draws.**
  - His **first draw** (ch 1) was an **EMERGENCY TRANSFER**. Nib was taken without being asked; that is a real wrong (log revealed ch 20).
  - From ch 21, draws are **invitations**: a contact window opens, one compatible adult candidate on the other side can answer or **decline**, and a refusal or timeout consumes the window (F04).
- **Route alignment** (the visible counter readers track).
  - Integrating an anchor fragment of a **new kind** raises **[ROUTE ALIGNMENT]**. At 100%, one contact window opens and the meter resets.
  - Repeat kinds add nothing (F05: no farming), so new biomes are the engine of adventure.
  - Early windows need two new kinds (+50% each). From the fourth window on, they need three (+34/33/33%).
  - Part One windows:
    - window 1: the free emergency first summon (Nib, ch 1);
    - window 2: stone (ch 19) + salt-iron (ch 22), DECLINED;
    - window 3: terrace clay + copper moss (ch 23), Orrin;
    - window 4: bridge-boss horn (ch 32) + exchange-gate sample (ch 41) + arcade glass (ch 45), Sera;
    - window 5: river stone (ch 63) + echo coral (ch 64) + a third river kind (ch 64), Kesh;
    - window 6: field ore and two Yeoju kinds (ch 84), Dara.
- **The link.**
  - Taeo feels pressure through a linked companion, as an amber pulse in his palm and ONLY when the companion permits it.
  - It works across a gate threshold for seconds at a time (ch 13).
- **[LOAD SHARE]** (from ch 29).
  - He can route a bounded fraction of mechanical impact between two willing linked people, and into himself.
  - Early loss is high. The direction must be prepared. Overload recoils as pain and bruising, and nosebleeds at worst.
  - It handles **mechanical impact and pressure only**: not heat, poison or signal deception (v3 boundary).
- **Growth (v3 ladder):**
  - feeling one channel (10–20);
  - two-anchor diversion (24–33);
  - reservoir store-and-release device (48–62);
  - three-anchor hand-off (63–80);
  - two-threshold relays (88–100).
  - Later (Part Two and beyond): branch isolation, cancellation, lattice.
- **Taeo rarely enters dungeons.** His courage is shown outside: at breach lines, carrying the load, waiting through silence, and making the call.

### Institutions and companies (all invented)
- **Gate Management Agency (GMA).** Government; runs Gate Safety Korea and the Emergency Alert texts.
- **Ability Assessment Office (AAO).** Assessment and registration; Chae works here.
- **Kanghan Institute.** Private research guild with a state contract. It ran the Annex during the Long Night. Its liaison is **Dr. Pyo Jaesung**.
- **Seoul Meridian Guild.** Big, efficient, consolidating. Led by **Director Baek Hojin**.
- **Ryu Survey.** Minho's family's three-van survey guild, in debt to Bluebird.
- **Dokgo Contracting.** Bottom-of-chain porter subcontractor; boss **Dokgo Chul**.
- **Other companies:** QuickHaul (courier platform), SumEx, Bluebird Capital, HunterLink (rankings and fan app), KNN 24 (news), TTOK (messenger), Saebom Bank.
- **Places:**
  - **Hanbit Mart:** Taeo's store, Hapjeong-dong, Mapo-gu.
  - **Haeseong:** the grandparents' small port town, 3 hours away by bus. Its fishing grounds are fenced off by a **sea gate** that never closes.

## 6. The cast: wants, flaws, arcs, dreams

### Yoon Taeo (23): protagonist, operator
- **Want, Part One.** Money for his family, a base nobody can evict them from, and to be *good at something rare*. In his secret heart he wants to be the kind of person Seoryun was on that broadcast.
- **Flaw** (v3, kept). He confuses taking responsibility with keeping control. He speaks over people, decides for them, and bets too big when he smells a win. His fear is real and never fully cured; courage is acting while afraid.
- **Ghost.** His parents died in the Seogang camp collapse when he was 14 and Haru was 1. He watched Seoryun's broadcast on the grandparents' TV a week after the funeral.
- **Dream** (declared ch 20, mocked by Minho, defended by Nib). "I'll clear the hardest gate in this country without setting foot in it, and everyone I send in comes home."
- **Part One arc.**
  1. He refuses to sell a person (6).
  2. He backs someone else's judgement at a cost (15–17).
  3. He faces the wrong he did (21).
  4. He meets his idol and tells the truth (39–40).
  5. He loses fairly and innovates (51–60).
  6. He reclaims an insult (61).
  7. He nearly kills a friend by trusting a machine over her (71), and apologises properly (75).
  8. He builds a guild whose door is open (100).
  9. His control flaw is still alive for Part Two.
- **Voice.** Polite, tired, dry. He says "sorry" too much, gets funnier when scared, and calls his brother "boss".
- **Props.** The olive backpack with the faded orange **team patch**. A cracked phone with Haru's dinosaur sticker.

### Nib: adult goblin scout, the first companion
- **Want.** To go back to his post, where someone (**Kett**, the younger scout he trained) "will find it empty and go looking". Underneath: to decide where he stands.
- **Flaw.** Distrust. He won't admit attachment, and he can be wrong about people.
- **Dream** (ch 31, to Orrin, at night). "One day I walk home on my own feet. Through a door I chose."
- **Arc.**
  1. He chooses to stay, for now (2).
  2. He speaks for himself at the review, "I'M SOMEONE'S RELIEF" (9).
  3. He earns and buys his own binoculars (19).
  4. He rages at the consent log and stays as a partner, not a possession (21).
  5. He becomes Orrin's unlikely best friend (25+).
  6. A crowd's goblin slurs hurt him, and Seoryun honours him by name (39).
  7. He hates fame (55).
  8. A false return signal fails because "THERE WAS NO KNOCK" (74).
  9. He disagrees with Taeo's big bet and still goes first (95).
  10. He becomes the guild's Scout Lead (100).
- **Voice.** Short declaratives, no small talk, deadpan, and unexpectedly kind with his hands.
- **Runners.**
  - "YOU FIRST." (makes others drink or step first)
  - He refuses shoes until he chooses boots himself (ch 43).
  - He loves pockets.
  - He calls Taeo's phone "the shouting brick".
  - He carves Kett's mark, a little three-line fork, on things he cares about.

### Orrin: adult stoneback carrier (arrives ch 24)
- **Look.** Tortoise-like, 2.3 m long, shell like a hill of stacked slate, a slow gentle face.
- **Personality.** Dry humour and huge spatial memory. Slow on purpose.
- **Want.** An inhabited home with a roof garden: "a place that doesn't move".
- **Hates** being called equipment.
- **Dream.** "A garden on a roof, with people under it who argue about dinner."
- **Arc.** He is the "wrong" result for the job and turns out to be its answer (33). He and Nib, smallest and largest, become friends. In ch 99 he gets the roof.

### Sera: adult human archer from a river settlement (arrives ch 46)
- **Personality.** Competitive, musical (a bone flute), vain about her skill, and honest about liking money and applause.
- **Want.** Her younger brother **Ilan**'s whereabouts, after he left home for "responsibilities".
- **Dream.** "To make a shot so good they sing it in both worlds."
- **Arc.** She rejects Taeo turning every job into a rescue at her expense (47). She loves the cameras (55). She is nearly killed by Taeo's interface-trust (71). The stored-wave shot wins the river (78). A dated message from Ilan points to a refuge (80).

### Kesh: adult moth-like signal keeper (arrives ch 65)
- **Personality.** Reads distant pulses; low endurance; noisy fields hurt him. He adores crowds, festivals and gossip anyway.
- **Want.** To rebuild a lost communication craft and train an apprentice.
- **Dream.** "A festival so loud both worlds hear it."
- **Arc.** He becomes co-designer of the relay plan (76–78) and the person who notices the echo (67).

### Dara: adult armoured field surgeon (arrives ch 85)
- **Personality.** Blunt, funny, abrasive. Her healing closes wounds, but does not replace blood or erase exhaustion.
- **Want.** To stop counting the ones she couldn't save.
- **Arc in Part One.** She critiques the crew's field practice brutally (86), learns from Sejin (90), and joins the founding (100). Her fusion with Tal is Part Two.

### Family
- **Yoon Haru (10).**
  - Dinosaurs, spicy seaweed snacks, loud.
  - He wants reliable time with Taeo, not money.
  - Taeo misses his school dinosaur presentation (ch 59) and goes to the re-run (ch 96) instead of a press event.

  - Studying for a food-business licence.

  - Freelance; wants a clinic with predictable hours. Insists "SUMMONED IS NOT A DIAGNOSIS."

### The six S-ranks (v3, kept)

  - Courteous and patient, and truly curious.

- **The bidding system** (arc IV), not a person. Its face is a Meridian contracts officer who is just doing his job.
- **BangBang (Bang Seokho), streamer.** Makes the "HIS SUMMONS GO FIRST!" clip (arc V).
- **The river predator.** Echo-mimicking boss (arc VI).
- **Iron Vow Guild**, the stronger rival in the anchor-field contest, and **Baek Hojin's** merger pressure (arc VII).
- **Venn.** Unstable-route broker. Seen only through the defects of his modified anchors in Part One (F12, from ch 88), and Part Two's first great enemy.

## 10. Chapter craft rules (every script)
1. **Length:** 45–65 panels, a 4–7 minute read.
2. **Cold open:** 2–6 panels before the title card. It must pose a question or deliver a striking image.
3. **Hook:** the final panel is a hook (reveal, threat, line or face), in a tall or full-width panel.
4. **Change:** each chapter changes the situation. Explaining, arranging or waiting is not enough: something must be decided, won, lost or revealed.
5. **Price tag:** at least one concrete number per chapter (won, minutes, %, cm).
6. **Camera and acting:** at least 3 silent panels; at least one big reaction; at least one comedy face (ART_BIBLE A5).
7. **Bubble types:** speech is default. Use the special types from ART_BIBLE B3 deliberately, and use the phone-voice bubble for every phone, radio or TV voice.
8. **System windows:** used sparingly and kept powerful. A chapter with a window lets it land with "DING" and space around it.
9. **Arc ends:** the last chapter of an arc resolves its local problem, makes an irreversible choice, and changes a relationship (v3 rule).
