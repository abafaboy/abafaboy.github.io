# Why our chapters 1–13 don't feel like a manhwa (measured)

**What was compared.**
- **Ours:** the scripts in `scripts/ch001–ch013.yaml` and the lettered strips (contact sheets of ch 1 and ch 10 were viewed).
- **Real:** the episodes in `MANHWA_CORPUS.md` and the rules in `MANHWA_CRAFT_BIBLE.md`.
- **Method.** Counts come from the YAML by script. The talk and action split is approximate: a panel counts as "talk-only" if it has lines and no SFX, and as "combat" if its art brief uses fight words.

## The numbers

| Measure | Ours (ch 1–13) | Real manhwa | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Talk-only panels | **69%** of 747 panels (56–80% per chapter; ch 10 was 80%) | Launch arcs alternate talk with danger, and fight episodes are mostly action. LN6: about 80 of 169 slices are the fight. | Too much talking |
| Chapters with a monster fight on the page | **0 of the first 9.** The first gate crossing is the last panels of ch 10, and the MC stays outside. | Danger in ch 1 in every Korean launch read (ORV2, LN1, MSCH1). A fight by ch 1–3. | The genre promise is missing for 10 chapters |
| System windows | **8 in 13 chapters**: 6 in ch 1, 2 in ch 2, then none in ch 3–13 | Every reward, rule and penalty is shown (LN2 and LN6 have one every few pages) | No visible growth |
| Won amounts in scripts | **122** (about 9 a chapter) | Money appears as one number when it matters (MSCH2: S-rank wealth; ORV2: "300 coins") | Money talk crowds out story |
| Words per bubble | **5.3** on average (ch 2: 4.0) | **~8–9** (ORV2 7.8, MSCH1 9.0) | Clipped lines, which the owner found confusing |
| Title card | Always panel 5 | 15–40% into the chapter, after the hook (ORV ~25%, MSCH2 42%, Eleceed 40%) | Mechanical |
| Summons in Part One | 5 in 100 chapters (ch 1, 24, 46, 65, 85) | MSCH titles mark a new companion at eps 9, 14, 26, 44, 56, 57 (about every 10); LN has 4 minions by ep 6 | Roster grows too slowly |
| End hooks, ch 1–13 | Mostly admin or quiet lines ("IT SAYS I HAVE THREE DAYS", "I DIDN'T ASK YOU TO", a news report) | Threat, mystery, system message, arrival, power reveal, countdown | Low pull to the next chapter |

## What this looks like on the page

1. **The stakes are paperwork.**
   - Ch 2–9 are about Nib's registration category, a 72-hour notice, a classification review, a ₩50,000,000 offer and day rates.
   - Ch 4 is 61 panels in an assessment office: "THE FORM HAS TWO BOXES. THE LAW BEHIND IT HAS THREE."
   - Real launch arcs stake lives: "Penalty for failure: Death" (ORV2).
2. **Exposition is lectured.**
   - Ch 10 stops for a 6-panel safety film about return marks.
   - Ch 4 explains a 1995 law.
   - Real titles hide rules in chaos (LORE DURING CHAOS, MSCH1), in a failed example (ORV2's burned money), or in a TV segment played for laughs (MSCH2).
3. **The power is passive and invisible.**
   - Taeo's ability is feeling pressure in his palm, tested with foam pads and sensors (ch 4), with a nosebleed as the first "win" (ch 12).
   - Readers of the genre expect to see growth "at a glance": numbers, cards, new skills.
4. **The MC is careful, apologetic and reactive.**
   - He says "SORRY" constantly by design.
   - He is never shown out-thinking anyone in ch 1–13.
   - Real "weak but smart" leads show the plan behind the face (MSCH2's grin), predict and are proven right (ORV3), or turn knowledge into spectacle (TGED1's blueprint).
5. **The camera is static.**
   - Contact sheets of ch 1 and ch 10 show mostly eye-level medium shots of people standing or sitting and talking in shops, offices and yards.
   - There are few extreme angles and few full-bleed impact panels.
   - The 13 chapters have 147 SFX in all. Only 26 are styled as impacts; 59 are "quiet" taps, buzzes and knocks.
6. **The social themes are arc spines.**
   - The design put automation, statelessness, subcontracting, jeonse fraud, disaster pricing and monopoly at the centre of the arcs (STORY_BIBLE §4).
   - Korean PDs warn that heavy content and slow build-ups lose readers (K46, K47).
   - The themes can stay as texture. They can't be the engine.

## What was good and should survive

- **The ch 1 ending.** The first summon appears and speaks ("WHERE AM I?"). That matches the genre's best launch ending (LN1).
- **Nib's deadpan voice** and the "YOU FIRST." running gag.
- **The power ideas**, which are strong once they are made visible:
  - a link through which the MC carries his summons' impact;
  - a reservoir, relay and lattice ladder;
  - summons who are people and can say no.
- **The art pipeline** is clean and consistent: Codex characters, a cyan UI kit, Anime Ace lettering.
