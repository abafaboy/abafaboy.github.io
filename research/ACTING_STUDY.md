# Acting study: how a real summoner manhwa poses bodies, acts faces and stages talk

**Why.** The owner's verdict on the last manhwa (25 October): "the biggest issue … was the body positions, emotions and dialogue panels. they were really unnatural and ai". `STORY_DIGEST.md` 7.5–7.6 measured the same faults in the old chapters:
- a narrow range of faces (a mild frown, mild worry, a small smile);
- eye-level medium shots with the subject centred;
- the same three camera set-ups repeated.

This study looks at a real summoner manhwa the owner chose, to see what it does instead.

**Source.**
- *Fighting Monsters with Monsters* (Korean webtoon; also listed as "Monsters are stopped by monsters" and similar).
- Seen through the owner's link: the YouTube recap "He Activated a System That Summons Monsters From a Game He Beat Into Our World! | Manhwa Recap" by Tyler's Manhwa, 3:46:31 long. The video names the title, and a viewer comment says it covers chapters 1–30.
- The premise is close to ours: a weak-rank hunter who streams a game gains the power to summon its monsters. A small goblin summon walks at his hip.

**Method.**
- Watched in Claude's built-in browser on 25 October 2026.
- About 50 moments were sampled between 0:55 and 2:10:00, and frames were viewed one or two at a time. About 70 panels with characters were judged; action and effects panels were skipped.
- The channel had blanked all speech text. The bubbles' size and position still show.
- Nothing was saved or downloaded. No frame from this title is ever given to Codex or to image generation.
- Timestamps are video time, so anyone can check them. Counts below are approximate tallies over those ~70 panels.

---

## 1. Bodies

| What it does | Where (video time) |
|---|---|
| **Weight is never even.** Standing figures put their weight on one leg, with hips and shoulders tilted against each other. A walking woman crosses one foot in front of the other. | 34:20 (the party walks toward camera), 109:48 (a woman stands with her hands in her coat pockets, weight on one hip) |
| **The body reacts, not just the face.** He is thrown back in his chair with his legs up and catches himself on the floor. He flops onto his bed with his arms flung out (SFX 털썩, "plop"). He hunches forward gripping his hair. | 10:00, 12:00, 13:30 |
| **Thinking poses use the hands.** Elbows on the desk and fingers laced in front of his mouth; a hand pressed to the forehead. | 13:00, 0:55 |
| **People touch things.** In about 20 of 70 panels a hand is on something: a keyboard and mouse, glasses being pushed up, a cheek being scratched, a handshake, a palm on the crystal, a finger tapping the status window, a fist, a pointing finger, hands in pockets. | 2:30, 10:30, 15:30, 35:40, 45:00, 60:44, 70:00, 130:00 |
| **Groups are staggered.** People stand at different depths and overlap, with heads at different heights. Each has their own stance: arms folded, hands on hips, a weapon resting on the ground, leaning. No lineups. | 34:20, 35:00 (a high wide shot of the party around a fallen ogre), 60:32 (a room of people in six different stances) |
| **Height difference is staged in the frame.** The small goblin summon stands in the lower corner looking up as he speaks; the hero looks down at him. A giant summon looms behind the hero, cropped by the top of the panel. | 111:36, 111:12, 112:00 |

## 2. Faces

| What it does | Where |
|---|---|
| **A wide ladder of emotion inside one chapter:** a sly half-smile; a sidelong glance; a grin with gritted teeth and sweat; eyes wide with small pupils; a scream with the mouth wide open. | 59:20, 36:20, 2:00, 10:30, 10:00 |
| **Comedy faces are simplified, not chibi bodies:** blank white eyes, dot eyes, an anger vein, a scratch of the cheek on a dotted pattern background. About 7 of 70 panels. | 2:00, 12:00, 15:30, 45:00, 120:00 |
| **Sweat drops are everywhere** (about 12 of 70 panels): for nerves, awkwardness and strain. | 0:55, 14:00, 36:00, 59:44, 110:12 |
| **Hidden eyes:** glasses turned to white glare on a nervous clerk; dark shadow over the eyes for dread. | 59:44, 13:30, 70:00 |
| **Extreme close-ups** of only an eye, a mouth, or a chin with sweat carry the decisive beats (about 8 of 70 panels). | 16:00, 16:30, 34:40, 45:00, 111:00, 120:00 |
| **Expressions are asymmetric:** one brow up, one corner of the mouth, one eye narrower. | 36:00, 59:20, 110:36 |

## 3. Talk panels

| What it does | Where |
|---|---|
| **Speakers look at each other, not at us.** Talk faces are three-quarter or profile, and the eyes go off-panel toward the other person. Faces aimed straight at the camera appear almost only in shock and comedy reactions, and in presentation shots (a hero walking toward us). | 36:20, 60:00, 59:44 against 10:30, 12:00 |
| **Over the listener's shoulder.** The listener's shoulder or the back of their head fills a corner, cropped; the speaker sits in the middle distance. Seen in about 11 of 70 panels; a further ~9 show a character from behind. | 36:00, 36:40, 59:32, 60:32, 110:00, 112:00 |
| **Arrivals and confrontations** are framed between two figures seen from behind in the foreground. | 109:48 (with footstep SFX 저벅저벅, "step step"), 110:00 |
| **Every exchange cuts between shot sizes:** a wide shot (who is where) → a two-shot → alternating close-ups → a hand or prop insert → a reaction → an extreme close-up on the line that matters. | 59:20–60:44 (the reception scene), 109:36–111:12 |
| **Bold crops:** heads cut at the top of the panel, eyes cut by the border, faces pushed to one edge with a shoulder filling the rest. A full figure centred with even headroom appears only in establishing shots. | 60:08, 60:32, 36:00 |
| **High and low cameras are common** (each in about 1 panel in 10): looking down on a desk, a reception counter or a group; looking up at a confident speaker. | 0:55, 59:32, 35:00; 34:00, 59:20, 60:20 |
| **Bubbles sit at the panel's top and bottom edges**, often breaking the border, with tails reaching in to the mouth. Two stacked faces can share one tall narrow panel with a bubble between them. | 34:00, 110:24 |

## 4. Small sound effects for small movements

The art letters tiny movements and feelings, small and near the action. English releases translate them as words:
- 힐끔 (GLANCE), 35:00;
- 멈칫 (FLINCH or FREEZE), 34:00;
- 움찔 (TWITCH), 2:00;
- 찰랑 (an earring swinging), 36:00;
- 씨익 (GRIN), 110:36;
- 후아 / 하아 (SIGH), 111:00, 11:30;
- 저벅저벅 (STEP STEP), 109:48;
- 털썩 (PLOP), 12:00;
- 타닥 (TAP TAP, typing), 2:30;
- 휑 (WHOOSH of empty wind), 111:12.

Our old chapters had eight SFX in about 355 panels (`STORY_DIGEST.md` 7.11).

## 5. Panel shape follows the shot
- **Tall and narrow:** a full figure, or two faces stacked (34:20, 110:24).
- **Wide:** a two-shot, a walk, or a group (35:40, 60:32).
- **Small and square:** a reaction (15:30, 36:20).

## 6. Against our old chapters, in one line each
- **Bodies:** square, symmetrical, arms hanging, hands idle → weight shifted, hands busy, bodies reacting.
- **Faces:** one mild range → a ladder from a sidelong glance to a scream, plus simplified comedy faces and sweat.
- **Talk:** eye-level, centred, both facing camera → three-quarter faces, eyelines to each other, over-the-shoulder, bold crops, and a new shot size every panel.
- **SFX:** almost none → tiny movement SFX on reactions.

## 7. What this study does not prove
- **[Inferred]** That image generation will pose figures this way when told to. The acting test (`codex/ACTING_TEST_BRIEF.md`) checks that.
- **[Inferred]** Passing a model sheet as a reference may pull a panel toward the sheet's neutral standing pose. The prompt kit now says the pose comes from the scene text (`MANHWA_STYLE_GUIDE.md` 10.5); the test will show whether that is enough.

The rules drawn from this study are in `codex/MANHWA_STYLE_GUIDE.md` 6b (acting and staging) and 11c (acting checks), and in the new `acting`, `eyeline` and `foreground` fields of `SCRIPT_FORMAT_v2.md`.
