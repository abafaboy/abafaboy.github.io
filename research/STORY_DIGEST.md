## 1. Premise, setting, tone and genre (as shown)

**Premise.** Taeo is a young grocery-store clerk who sends money to his grandmother; his little brother is with her on their video calls. A gate opens inside his store. After he helps get customers out, system windows appear for him: "[AWAKENING COMPLETE]", "[CLASS: SUMMONER]" and "[FIRST SUMMON AVAILABLE]". At home he spends his one draw out of curiosity, saying first "I'M NOT GOING INTO A DUNGEON." and then "I JUST WANT TO SEE."

The summon is **Nib**, a goblin scout. Unlike the obedient summons of this world, he talks, remembers a life and wants to go home. The seven chapters follow the next four days:
- an uneasy first night;
- an official ability assessment;
- Taeo's shift, with Nib waiting in the break room;
- Nib's first paid job, scouting a practice yard for a survey crew.

**Setting.** A contemporary Korean city: prices in won, Korean-style studio flats, a noodle shop with floor seating. Gates, hunters and summons are ordinary public life here:
- police cordons;
- hunters interviewed on TV news;
- couriers who use summons as labour;
- an institution the phone lists as the "Ability assessment office";
- survey crews who record what is inside gates.

**Tone and genre.** This is grounded slice-of-life inside the Korean "hunter / gate / system" urban-fantasy frame, played deliberately low-key: no fights, no levelling and no villains in seven chapters. The emotional core is consent and personhood (the summon is a person) and the small economics of a working life:
- a ₩24,800 balance;
- swapping a shift to see family;
- "ABOUT TWO OF MY LUNCHES".

The title *My Summons Go First* reads like a power fantasy. So far, "go first" means Nib literally walking ahead to scout while Taeo learns to let him finish his sentences.

## 2. Characters

### Taeo (protagonist)

**Role.** Grocery-store clerk, newly awakened Summoner, and Nib's summoner.

**Look.**
- Tall and slim with very long legs.
- Messy near-black hair (reads dark brown in chapter 1) and brown eyes.
- Black hoodie and black cargo trousers.
- At work, a grey-green utility vest with a blank white name tag.
- Outdoors (chapters 3–7), a navy zip jacket and chunky black sneakers.
- Nearly always has his phone in hand.

**Relationships.**
- He transfers money to his grandmother; the phone shows "Grandma / Transfer sent / Available balance ₩24,800".
- He video-calls his little brother at lunch.
- He is on easy terms with his coworker.
- A framed photo of four people hangs in his flat and is never explained. No parents are mentioned.

**Personality.**
- Tired, polite and apologetic ("I'M SORRY I BROUGHT YOU HERE.").
- Careful: "DON'T TOUCH THAT." to a customer reaching for the crack, and he gets the elderly customer out first.
- Curious rather than ambitious.
- He tends to answer for Nib and to cut him off. The story treats this as his flaw: "YOU DIDN'T ASK ME." / "NO. I DIDN'T." in chapter 3, and "I CUT YOU OFF." in chapter 6.

**What he wants.**
- To get through his shifts and see his family on Sunday.
- To stay out of gates. He texts Chae: "Is there any work I can do without going into a gate?"
- To help Nib home.
- To be good at guiding: "I WANTED TO BE GOOD AT IT."
- He has watched S-rank hunter Han Seoryun "FOR YEARS" because of a news rescue from his childhood: "SHE KEPT GOING BACK FOR PEOPLE. … I LIKED THAT PART."

**Ability as shown.**
- The assessor's gauge reads "YOUR OUTPUT IS LOW", and no rank is assigned yet.
- He intermittently feels pressure in his palm when Nib presses something ("LIKE SOMEONE PRESSING IT"), even when he is not watching.
- He cannot reopen the system window.
- His assessment sheet reads "Low output. Intermittent sensory response."

### Nib, Goblin Scout (the summon)

**Label at summoning.** "[NIB — GOBLIN SCOUT]".

**Look.**
- Olive-green skin, spiky black hair, long pointed ears and a long pointed nose.
- A small fang shows in some close-ups.
- Eyes are yellow-amber in chapters 1–2 and brown in later close-ups.
- Torn pinkish-tan sleeveless wrap top and torn charcoal shorts; barefoot.
- Rope belt (brown in chapters 1–2, orange in chapters 5–7) carrying a leather drawstring pouch, which later holds his money, and a knife in a brown wooden sheath.
- From chapter 2 onward he is drawn at about thigh-to-hip height beside adults. At the chapter 1 reveal he is drawn close to human size (see 7.4).

**Personality.**
- Wary, terse, proud, practical and observant.
- Makes Taeo drink first ("YOU FIRST.") and keeps a hand near his knife.
- Refuses to be treated as a commanded summon: "I'M NOT THAT ONE."
- Objects to being discussed in front of him: "I CAN HEAR YOU."
- Spots a failing box before anyone else: "I COULD SEE THE BOTTOM."
- Pays for his own lunch: "I'M BUYING MINE."
- Dry humour: "TOOK YOU LONG ENOUGH." and "THEN LET ME FINISH A SENTENCE."

**Abilities shown.**
- Watching and route-finding.
- Remembering terrain detail: "THE SPLIT. AND THE WHITE BIT HERE."
- Map drawing.
- Picking up the radio quickly.
- He speaks fluently from arrival and does not follow commands. No combat is shown.

**Backstory he gives.** Before arriving he was "KEEPING WATCH" alone at a post: a stone wall about chest height with a tree behind it, overlooking "THE LOWER PATH". He adds: "SOMEONE WAS COMING TO TAKE OVER." / "HE'D FIND MY PLACE EMPTY." / "HE'D GO LOOKING."

**What he wants.** First "SEND ME BACK." and to find his watch post. Later, to earn money and make his own choices, and to join Taeo's family visit: "CAN I COME?"

## 3. World rules shown so far

### Gates

- **How they open.** Without warning, in public places. In chapter 1 a thin dark crack in a store aisle widens into a tall dark column ("VMMMM.").
- **What follows.** Staff evacuate, police tape off the area and hunters go in.
  - Police: "AFTER THEY CLEAR THE BOSS AND THE GATE CLOSES. WE DON'T HAVE A TIME YET."
  - The next day: "GONE. THE HUNTERS CLEARED IT."
- **Inside.** Taeo calls what lies inside "A DUNGEON".
- **Status.** Gates have a recorded status. The phone shows "GATE STATUS: CLOSED / Recording retained". A closed gate cannot be visited, but its survey recording survives: "THE RECORDING STAYS. THE GATE DOESN'T."

### Hunters

- They are ranked (S-rank is shown), work in rotating teams and appear on TV news.

### Awakening and the system UI

The system text appears in navy rounded boxes with a cyan edge. Exact strings:
- "[AWAKENING COMPLETE]" and "[CLASS: SUMMONER]": drawn while Taeo is still inside the store near the exit.
- "[FIRST SUMMON AVAILABLE]": outside, and again as he walks home.
- At home, "[AVAILABLE DRAWS: 1]" and "[DRAW]".
- After he presses it, "[NIB — GOBLIN SCOUT]".

Afterwards he cannot call the window back: "I DON'T KNOW HOW TO BRING IT UP AGAIN."

Public information on his phone:
- "NEWLY AWAKENED?" / "Contact us to book an ability assessment."
- "Most summons follow simple commands. They aren't expected to speak."

### Summons

- **Common in daily life.** "PEOPLE SEE SUMMONS HERE." Examples are the courier's stone golem and the workers' wooden mannequins. They follow short commands; "gently" is beyond the golem.
- **Nib is the exception.** He speaks, remembers a life and refuses orders. Chae: "HIS RESPONSES DON'T MATCH WHAT I'D EXPECT FROM AN ORDINARY SUMMON."
- **The summoner link.**
  - It is measurable: both sensor readings change together.
  - Taeo feels as pressure what Nib touches, intermittently, even without watching.
  - Whether it works at a distance is untested ("WE HAVEN'T TESTED DISTANCE").
- **Dismissal.** It exists as an idea but is deliberately not tried.

### Institutions

- **Ability assessment office.**
  - Booked by phone; the assessment is in person and requires ID.
  - An assessor measures output with a hand plate and an analogue gauge, and the summoner–summon connection with wrist fobs and a monitor.
  - Rank is assigned later; a written assessment sheet is issued.
  - It gives access to "PUBLIC GATE SURVEYS", an archive of recordings from surveyed gates, and keeps an "arrival report" that Chae will "KEEP CHECKING".
- **Survey crews.** Minho's crew go into gates and make survey recordings.
  - They train in a fenced practice yard of movable plywood screens and use radios.
  - They hire for non-gate work by the hour.
  - Their next survey is "A DRAINAGE TUNNEL", underground.
- **Others.** Police run cordons; TV news covers hunters.

### Money (Korean won)

- Taeo's balance after sending money to Grandma: ₩24,800.
- Yard job: "ONE HOUR, TWENTY THOUSAND WON", which Taeo calls "ABOUT TWO OF MY LUNCHES".
- Noodles: ₩7,000. Nib pays with a ₩10,000 note and gets ₩3,000 back.

### Work

- The store rota is a whiteboard. Taeo's shift is "SIX HOURS" ("come in at four"), and the manager approves shift swaps.

## 4. Chapter-by-chapter summary

### Chapter 1: "One More Shift" (64 panels)

- **The shift.** Evening at a small grocery store. A customer argues an expired deal: "THESE WERE TWO FOR ONE." / "THAT ENDED YESTERDAY."
- **The courier.** He arrives with a stone golem that drops crates on the counter ("THUNK."). Taeo: "CAN IT DO "GENTLY"?" Courier: "STILL WORKING ON THAT. CARRY, FOLLOW, STOP. THAT'S MOST OF IT."
- **The break.** Taeo eats while video-calling his little brother, whom Grandma is feeding: "GRANDMA. I SAW THAT." / "GOOD. TELL THIS ONE TO EAT." His brother asks if he's coming Sunday, but the wall rota shows Taeo working Sunday: "I'LL ASK ABOUT SWAPPING." / "YOU SAID THAT LAST TIME." / "I KNOW." His phone confirms the transfer to Grandma and a balance of ₩24,800.
- **The gate.** His coworker calls him over ("TAEO? COME LOOK AT THIS.") to a thin dark crack in an aisle. He warns a customer, "DON'T TOUCH THAT.", and it widens into a gate ("VMMMM."). The coworker orders everyone out. Taeo escorts an elderly man, telling him to leave his basket ("LEAVE IT. WE'LL KEEP IT HERE.").
- **The awakening.** Near the exit the windows appear, "[AWAKENING COMPLETE]" and "[CLASS: SUMMONER]" (Taeo: "WHAT—"), and his coworker pulls him out. Outside there is police tape and hunters arrive and walk into the store. "[FIRST SUMMON AVAILABLE]" follows him home.
- **At home.** Messages arrive: "Grandma says thank you. She also says buy yourself actual food." / "Tell her I ate." So does an ad for assessments and the line that summons "aren't expected to speak." Remembering the golem, he decides: "I'M NOT GOING INTO A DUNGEON." "I JUST WANT TO SEE."
- **The draw.** He clears the floor and presses "DRAW". A goblin appears in blue light: "[NIB — GOBLIN SCOUT]". Taeo, palm out: "STAY." The goblin: "WHERE AM I?" The final panel is Taeo's stunned face.

### Chapter 2: "A Place to Sit" (61 panels)

- **Setting.** The same night, in one room.
- **Standoff.** "YOU CAN TALK." Nib demands answers with his hand on his knife and makes Taeo move away from the door.
- **Explanation.** Taeo explains the words and the button: "I WANTED TO SEE WHAT WOULD HAPPEN." "I DIDN'T KNOW IT WOULD BE SOMEONE." He cannot reopen the window: "SEND ME BACK." / "I DON'T KNOW HOW." / "THEN FIND OUT."
- **Names.** Nib learns his name came from the label ("IT WAS WRITTEN BESIDE YOU.") and asks for Taeo's: "YOUR NAME." / "TAEO." / "TAEO."
- **The window.** Nib looks out at an unfamiliar city: "DO YOU RECOGNIZE ANYTHING?" / "NO."
- **The water ritual.** Taeo sets a mug on the floor where Nib points, then has to drink first ("YOU FIRST."). A phone buzz ("BRR.") makes Nib spill. "IT'S MINE. JUST A MESSAGE." / "I WASN'T ASKING."
- **Apology.** "I'M SORRY I BROUGHT YOU HERE." / "YOU STILL DID." / "I KNOW." Taeo explains the hallway and street outside, then offers a seat. Nib sits against the bed: "HERE."
- **On "STAY".** Taeo: "I SAW A SUMMON AT WORK. IT ONLY FOLLOWS COMMANDS. I THOUGHT—" Nib: "I'M NOT THAT ONE." Taeo: "NO. YOU'RE NOT."
- **Truce.** "WOULD YOU RATHER WAIT HERE FOR A WHILE?" / "FOR A WHILE." Nib holds out his mug: "MORE WATER." The chapter ends with Taeo refilling it at the tap.

### Chapter 3: "Both of Us" (62 panels)

- **Night texts.** "The hunters are out. Nobody hurt. I'll let you know about tomorrow." Taeo explains the phone ("A PHONE. I CAN TALK TO PEOPLE WITH IT." / "PEOPLE WHO AREN'T HERE?") and the office: "THERE'S AN OFFICE FOR PEOPLE WHO WAKE UP WITH ABILITIES. … I'LL CALL WHEN THEY OPEN."
- **Sleeping arrangements.** Nib takes a blanket to the floor ("YOU SLEEP UP THERE?" / "THEN GO ON.") and stays sitting up while Taeo sleeps.
- **Morning call.** The office books them for eleven: "BRING YOUR IDENTIFICATION, AND BRING HIM WITH YOU." "DON'T TRY ANYTHING NEW BEFORE THE APPOINTMENT." Nib leans to the phone: "CAN YOU DO THAT?" A text arrives: "Manager says come in at four."
- **The walk.** Twenty minutes across the city. Nib hesitates at the street door, and they pass a worker with a mannequin summon: "THEY'RE NOT LOOKING AT IT." / "PEOPLE SEE SUMMONS HERE."
- **Waiting room.** The TV interviews Han Seoryun. Taeo: "NO. I'VE WATCHED HER FOR YEARS." … "SHE KEPT GOING BACK FOR PEOPLE." … "I LIKED THAT PART."
- **Assessment with Chae.**
  - Taeo recounts the gate and the draw.
  - The hand-plate gauge: "YOUR OUTPUT IS LOW." … "I'M NOT ASSIGNING YOUR RANK YET." Nib is not ordinary: "HIS RESPONSES DON'T MATCH WHAT I'D EXPECT FROM AN ORDINARY SUMMON."
  - Taeo guesses Nib came from a dungeon; Nib: "YOU DIDN'T ASK ME." Chae asks Nib, who was "KEEPING WATCH" and whose relief "HE'D FIND MY PLACE EMPTY." / "HE'D GO LOOKING."
- **Cliff.** Chae sets up a connection test: "TAEO, THAT MARK. NIB, OVER HERE." END.

### Chapter 4: "Something There" (55 panels)

- **Sensors.** Both wear sensor fobs. Chae: "YOU'RE LOOKING FOR A FEELING, NOT TRYING TO MAKE SOMETHING HAPPEN." Taeo feels pressure in his palm, and both traces blip: "BOTH READINGS CHANGED THERE."
- **Blind test.** Taeo faces the wall while Nib presses a stool cushion on Chae's cue.
  - Taeo feels it twice: "THERE." / "WHICH HAND?" / "MY LEFT." "I MEAN MINE." "I DON'T KNOW."
  - Then he doesn't: "NOTHING." / "I HAD IT." / "YOU DID. AND THIS TIME YOU DIDN'T."
- **No way back yet.** The system window still won't open. Nib: "BUT YOU CAN'T SEND ME BACK." Chae: "I CAN'T DO THAT WITH WHAT WE'VE FOUND."
- **The map.** Nib draws his post: "WALL HERE. TREE BEHIND IT." "I SAT HERE. YOU COULD SEE THE LOWER PATH." Chae brings up "PUBLIC GATE SURVEYS"; the first recording is wrong ("NOT THAT.").
- **Chae's instructions.** She will send the recordings and keep checking the arrival report. She warns against trying a dismissal and hands over the sheet: "Low output. Intermittent sensory response."
- **At the store.** The gate is gone. Taeo introduces Nib to his coworker ("THIS IS NIB. I AWAKENED YESTERDAY."), and Nib tells her "I CAN HEAR YOU." Via the coworker, the manager allows Nib in the break room: "JUST KEEP THIS DOORWAY CLEAR." / "IT IS CLEAR."
- **Ending.** Taeo shows him how to play the recordings and goes to work: "CALL ME IF YOU NEED—" / "I KNOW WHERE YOU ARE." END, on Nib alone with the phone and his drawing.

### Chapter 5: "The Way Around" (38 panels)

- **Watching.** Nib watches the recordings and asks for something to draw on; Taeo brings flattened cardboard. Watching a route loop around two split rocks, he says: "YOU CAME BACK AROUND."
- **The courier returns.** Nib, with his golem. Nib spots a box giving way ("THE BOTTOM'S COMING OUT.") and helps recover the spilled bags ("THUP. THUP."). Courier: "THANKS FOR SPOTTING THAT." Nib: "I COULD SEE THE BOTTOM."
- **What he noticed.** "I KEPT THINKING IT WAS SOMEWHERE NEW. THEY WENT AROUND THE SAME ROCK." / "THE SPLIT. AND THE WHITE BIT HERE."
- **Scrubbing.** Taeo teaches him to scrub back through a video: "SIX IN THIS LINK. THIS IS THE FIRST." / "YOU DON'T HAVE TO GET THROUGH ALL OF THEM TONIGHT." / "I WANT TO."
- **Shift swap.** The coworker gives Nib a pencil and agrees to swap shifts. At 5:40 Taeo catches the manager: "FINE. CHANGE IT ON THE SCHEDULE BEFORE YOU FORGET." He writes SUNDAY OFF on the board ("I CAN SEE MY FAMILY ON SUNDAY.") and texts "Got Sunday off. I'll come over."
- **Ending.** END, on Nib tracing the loop on his cardboard map.

### Chapter 6: "The Person Outside" (41 panels)

- **Texts with Chae.** "Is there any work I can do without going into a gate?" → "A crew supervisor can talk you through it. Minho runs a survey crew. West practice yard, ten today, if you can make it." → "We'll come."
- **At the yard.** Nib shows Minho his drawing: "HAVE YOU SEEN A PLACE LIKE THIS?" / "NOT ONE I RECOGNIZE."
- **The test.** Minho offers outside work but wants to see them work together first: get a cased load through the plywood maze, with Nib scouting by radio ("HOLD THIS TO SPEAK. LET GO TO LISTEN.").
- **First attempt fails** on a miscommunication: "I WENT LEFT. THE GAP AT THE END—" / "OKAY. COMING." … "I SAID I WENT LEFT." / "I CUT YOU OFF." Minho: "TAKE IT BACK. LET HIM FINISH LOOKING."
- **Second attempt works.** "TAKE THE RIGHT ROUTE. THERE'S ROOM ALL THE WAY THROUGH." Then: "WE GOT IT THROUGH." / "TOOK YOU LONG ENOUGH." / "IT DID. BUT YOU STOPPED GUESSING."
- **The job.** Minho hires Nib, not Taeo, to check the moved route the next morning: "ONE HOUR, TWENTY THOUSAND WON." Taeo asks to do the radio: "YOU NEED MORE PRACTICE BEFORE YOU GUIDE A CREW." / "YOU CAN WATCH. BRING THAT ASSESSMENT SHEET."
- **Walking out.** "YOU'RE ANNOYED." / "A BIT." / "I DIDN'T ASK HIM TO LEAVE YOU OUT." / "I KNOW. I WANTED TO BE GOOD AT IT." / "THEN LET ME FINISH A SENTENCE." Nib plans to ask where they are surveying next.
- **Ending.** END, on a text: "8:00 — yard. Bring assessment sheet."

### Chapter 7: "Twenty Thousand Won" (34 panels)

- **The job.** Nib walks the new route by radio and reports a hazard: "THERE'S CORD ACROSS THE WAY OUT. BY THE LAST SCREEN." / "IT GOES ROUND THE FOOT THERE, TOO."
- **Payment.** Minho pays two ₩10,000 notes: "TWENTY THOUSAND. AS AGREED." / "BOTH OF THESE?" / "BOTH."
- **Next survey.** "A DRAINAGE TUNNEL." It is underground, so Nib: "SO THEY WON'T SEE THIS."
- **The recording.** Minho checks the recording Nib recognised: "THIS GATE'S CLOSED." / "BUT THE PICTURE'S STILL THERE." / "THE RECORDING STAYS. THE GATE DOESN'T." The phone shows "GATE STATUS: CLOSED / Recording retained". Nib: "KEEP THIS ONE, THOUGH." "IF MY WALL'S IN AN OLD PICTURE, I STILL WANT TO KNOW."
- **Lunch.** At a noodle shop Nib buys his own bowl: "YOU DON'T HAVE TO—" / "I'M BUYING MINE." Over noodles he asks to come on Sunday: "CAN I COME?"
- **The call.** Taeo phones (by context, Grandma) and tells her the news:
  - "I AWAKENED THIS WEEK. I SUMMONED HIM." / "YOU WHAT?"
  - "WHY DIDN'T YOU TELL ME?" / "I DIDN'T KNOW HOW TO EXPLAIN IT."
  - "ARE YOU GOING INTO GATES?" / "NO."

## 5. Open threads, mysteries and setups

1. **Nib's origin.** The watch post (a stone wall about chest height, a tree behind it, a view of the lower path) and the unnamed relief who "HE'D FIND MY PLACE EMPTY. HE'D GO LOOKING.": someone on the other side is looking for him.
2. **The split-boulder recording.** Nib recognised the route going "AROUND THE SAME ROCK" and "THE WHITE BIT HERE". That gate is closed, with the recording retained, and Nib keeps it. The other five recordings in Chae's link are still unseen.
3. **Can Nib go back, and how?** Dismissal is untested and warned against ("WE HAVEN'T ESTABLISHED WHERE HE'D GO."). Minho says a place can be reached only "IF THE GATE'S STILL OPEN".
4. **Why Nib isn't an "ordinary summon".** Why he talks and remembers, and whether summons are drawn from real places or populations.
5. **Taeo's power.** Low output, no rank yet, an intermittent touch link with untested range, a system window that won't reopen, and whether more draws will come. The title says "Summons", plural.
6. **Chae's "arrival report".** This implies other arrivals are being tracked.
7. **The store gate.** Its "boss" was cleared off-panel. Is its opening linked to Taeo's awakening or to Nib?
8. **Work path.** Minho's "DISPATCH, ONCE YOU KNOW THE JOB.", Taeo wanting to guide a crew, and Nib scouting by radio. This is the most likely meaning of the title.
9. **The drainage-tunnel survey.** An underground location set up for later.
10. **Sunday at noon at Grandma's.** Nib meets the family; Grandma is afraid of gates ("ARE YOU GOING INTO GATES?"); the spicy seaweed snacks promised to the brother; "DOES HE EAT FISH?"
11. **Han Seoryun.** Taeo's childhood hero is set up on TV, which points to a later meeting.
12. **Money.** Taeo's ₩24,800 after the transfer, and Nib now earning money of his own.
13. **The framed photo of four people** in Taeo's flat. Parents are never mentioned.

## 6. Story critique

### What works

- **A fresh angle on a crowded genre.** Awakening and summoner stories usually sell power. Here the summon is a person who was in the middle of a job, and the drama is consent: Taeo's "STAY." is answered by "WHERE AM I?", and chapter 2 is a negotiation over a mug of water. That inversion is the series' best idea, and the title quietly turns it around: the summon goes first because he is the better scout.
- **Nib is the strongest character.** He has a consistent voice (short declaratives, no small talk), clear wants, and visible movement from hostility to choosing to join Taeo's family ("CAN I COME?"). His autonomy beats land: "I CAN HEAR YOU.", "I'M NOT THAT ONE." and "I'M BUYING MINE."
- **Economical, natural dialogue, with honest small payoffs.**
  - The Sunday swap set up in chapter 1 ("YOU SAID THAT LAST TIME.") pays off in chapter 5 ("Got Sunday off.") and chapter 7 ("BOTH OF US. NOON.").
  - The maze miscommunication ("I SAID I WENT LEFT.") pays off in "THEN LET ME FINISH A SENTENCE."
  - Food as care runs through every chapter: "ARE YOU EATING?" → "buy yourself actual food" → "THEN EAT. YOURS IS GOING COLD."
- **World-building by procedure.** Bookings, gauges, survey archives, gate status and hourly rates do the work instead of exposition dumps, so the world feels administered and plausible.
- **Clean causality.** Every scene follows from the previous one, and nothing is contrived.

### What is thin or generic

- **Stakes are close to zero for seven chapters.** The only danger, the gate, is handled off-panel by hunters in chapter 1. No antagonist, deadline or cost appears. Nib's relief who will "GO LOOKING" is the one emotional stake, and it is not developed. Money pressure (₩24,800) is shown once and never bites.
- **Weak chapter-end hooks.** Only chapter 1 ends on a real hook ("WHERE AM I?"); chapter 3 cuts just before a test begins, which is a mild one. Chapters 2 and 4–7 end on quiet beats: refilling a mug, Nib alone with a phone, tracing a loop, a text message, "YOURS IS GOING COLD." In a weekly vertical format that gives readers little reason to open the next chapter.
- **Taeo is under-written.** He reacts, apologises and complies. There are no narration or thought captions anywhere in seven chapters, so his inner life is never voiced. Untouched so far:
  - what it means to him to be a low-output Summoner;
  - his ambitions and fears;
  - why he supports Grandma, and where his parents are.
  
  His admiration for Han Seoryun is told in one exchange and then dropped.
- **Genre furniture without payoff.** S-rank, classes, "draws", system windows and bosses are invoked, but none is explored. The draw mechanic in particular has no rules yet.
- **A functional, mostly unnamed supporting cast.** "Coworker" (literally her contact name), "Manager", Grandma, the brother and the courier have no names. Chae, Minho and Taeo share the same clipped register, so their voices blur.
- **Repetitive verbal rhythm.** "ALL RIGHT." "I KNOW." "YES." "RIGHT." and "OKAY." recur constantly. Scenes run on short call-and-response, which flattens their emotional range.
- **Low plot density.** Seven chapters cover about four days and three main locations:
  - chapter 2 is a single conversation spread over 61 panels;
  - chapters 3–4 are one assessment that ends with "we don't know yet";
  - chapter 6 is a ten-minute exercise.
  
  The intimacy is intentional, but no scene has yet changed the situation in a big way.
- **Small contradictions.** Taeo tells Chae "AFTER WE GOT OUTSIDE, I SAW THE CLASS MESSAGE" and tells his coworker "AFTER WE GOT OUT", but chapter 1 draws "[AWAKENING COMPLETE]" / "[CLASS: SUMMONER]" while he is still inside by the exit (`ch/001/r2-p033.webp`). The title of chapter 6, "The Person Outside", doesn't map to an obvious event in it.

### Character development and stakes, in one line

Nib develops; Taeo moves a little (learning to listen, telling Grandma). The relationship is the spine and it works, but it moves slowly and nothing threatens it.

## 7. Art, lettering and pacing critique

Labels used below: **[Measured]** means counted or checked on the pages. **[Observed]** means seen while reading, with the location given. **[Judgement]** is my assessment. **[Convention, unverified]** means general professional practice that I could not source in this session.

### 7.1 Two different productions

- **[Observed] Chapters 1–2** (800 px webp segments):
  - one landscape panel per segment;
  - softer, slightly blurry rendering and a muted palette;
  - bubbles set over empty headroom at the top of each image;
  - the reader adds 64 px white gutters;
  - no title card and no end card.
- **[Observed] Chapters 3–7** (1200 px pages):
  - crisper, brighter, more saturated rendering;
  - a title header ("My Summons Go First / Chapter N: …") and an "END CHAPTER N" card in plain sans;
  - most dialogue moved into white bands between the panels.
- **[Judgement]** A reader going from chapter 2 to chapter 3 sees the format, palette and lettering method change all at once.
- **[Observed] Patched artefact.** The reader masks the top 33 px of `ch/001/r2-p037.webp`; underneath is a leftover black band from page assembly.

### 7.2 Line art and colouring

- **[Observed]**
  - Thin outlines of even, uniform weight, with soft cel shading over painterly, near-photographic backgrounds.
  - No variation in line weight for depth or emphasis.
  - No screentone, speed lines or effect lines anywhere in seven chapters.
  - Chapters 3–7 are evenly lit daylight; chapter 1's dusk and night scenes have a cooler cast.
  - The key beats (the awakening, the summoning) get no mood lighting beyond a blue glow on the floor.
- **[Observed]** At its native 800 px, chapter 1 is soft: packaging and shelf detail smear when zoomed (`ch/001/r2-p001.webp`).
- **[Observed] Colour-model break.** On chapter 4 pages 12–14, Nib's skin turns orange-tan and the room's walls saturate to bright blue. On pages 1–3 of the same scene he is olive-green against pale walls (crop 5).

### 7.3 Backgrounds

- **[Judgement]** The backgrounds are the strongest part of the art. The store, break room, office, yard and noodle shop are detailed, correct in perspective and easy to read. They are photographic set dressing rather than drawn space, though: every panel has the same density of detail, with no simplification in quiet beats.
- **[Observed] Taeo's flat changes design.**
  - Chapter 1: bed on storage drawers with a grey duvet, a clothes rack, a folding table.
  - Chapters 2–3: a wooden bed frame with white sheets; chapter 3's morning adds a glass balcony door and blue curtains.
  - Chapter 6: a modern studio with a wide window, a balcony rail, city towers and a wooden table.
  
  The break room stays broadly consistent across chapters 1, 4 and 5.
- **[Observed] No legible drawn text.** Packaging, signage and name tags are blank or smudged. All readable text (system boxes, phone screens, the TV caption, the whiteboard rota, banknote numbers) is overlaid digitally.

### 7.4 Consistency of faces and character designs

**[Observed]** Within chapters 3–7, faces stay fairly stable for Taeo, Nib, Chae, Minho and the coworker. Breaks found, with locations:

- **Nib's scale.** At the reveal (`ch/001/r2-p059.webp`, `r2-p060.webp`) he stands about three-quarters of door height, close to human size. From chapter 2 on he is thigh-to-hip height.
- **Nib's eyes.** Yellow-amber in chapters 1–2, brown in later close-ups.
- **Nib's belt and knife.** The rope is brown in chapters 1–2 and orange in chapters 5–7. In chapter 5 the knife sheath is drawn as a black cylinder with a silver cap, like a thermos (crop 2). In chapters 6–7 it is a brown wooden sheath (crop 1).
- **Nib's skin.** Orange-tan on chapter 4 pages 12–14.
- **Taeo's feet.**
  - Chapter 1: white socks in `r2-p053` and `r2-p057`, then white sneakers in `r2-p060`.
  - Chapter 3: black sneakers on pages 11–20, brown leather boots in the office on page 21, then black again.
- **Relative heights.** Taeo is drawn with fashion-model leg length and towers over Chae in some panels (the final panel of chapter 3) but not in others.
- **System UI and gate effects.**
  - The DRAW prompt is a navy box with a cyan edge in `ch/001/r2-p055`, and a glowing translucent blue plate in the very next panel, `r2-p056`.
  - The gate is a textured dark column in `r2-p024` and a flat, curtain-like navy rectangle across the aisle in `r2-p028`.
- **Phone UI.**
  - The same sent message ("Home now. Thanks. Let me know when you get back.") is a left-aligned blue bubble in the inset on chapter 3 page 1, and right-aligned on chapter 3 page 30.
  - In `ch/001/r2-p047` the top half of the phone screen is blank white.

### 7.5 Expressions and acting

- **[Judgement] Narrow range.** Mostly a mild frown, mild worry or a small smile. The big beats (the awakening, the goblin appearing, Grandma's "YOU WHAT?") get the same restrained faces. The largest reaction in seven chapters is Taeo's stunned close-up that ends chapter 1.
- **No comedic distortion or exaggerated reactions**, which this genre normally uses to vary rhythm [Convention, unverified].
- **What works.** Nib's scowl is well done and consistent, and hand acting improves in chapters 5–7: paying, tying the pouch, holding the drawing.

### 7.6 Composition and camera

- **[Measured] Repeated set-ups.**
  - Chapter 2: about 21 of 61 panels are full-room wide shots from roughly three camera positions (door in the centre, Taeo on one side, Nib on the other).
  - Chapter 7: the last 11 panels, from the lower panel of page 12 to the end, all use the same three-quarter view of the low table with only small reframing. That is about a third of the chapter's 34 panels (crop 6).
- **[Observed] Camera habits.** Almost always eye level, medium to wide, subject centred. Extreme close-ups are rare, and high, low or tilted angles almost never appear.
- **Exceptions that work.** The overhead maze shots in chapter 6 (crop 3), the walkie-talkie push-to-talk close-ups, and the over-the-shoulder video call in chapter 1.

### 7.7 Panel sizes and gutters

- **[Measured] Chapter 1.** Every segment is one full-width landscape panel 473–922 px tall (at 800 px wide), separated by an identical 64 px gutter. The rhythm is metronomic.
- **[Measured] Chapters 3–7.** The artwork blocks are mostly full width, with a median height of about 800 px at 1200 px wide, roughly 3:2 landscape. There are a few tall panels (up to about 1,800–1,970 px) and a handful of insets and split panels.
- **[Measured] White space.** Only 60–66% of the scroll height in chapters 3–7 is artwork (81–84% in chapters 1–2). The rest is white space, mostly holding bubbles.
- **[Observed] Missing panel types.** No bleed panels, broken borders, diagonal or irregular panels, and no edge-to-edge reveal panel. Nib's arrival and the gate opening are both standard-size rectangles.

### 7.8 How the action reads

- **[Observed]** The little action there is (the golem unloading, a box bottom giving way, rice bags falling, the maze run) is legible because the staging is wide and clear. It still reads as a series of still photographs: no motion lines, impact frames, blur or effects.
- **[Observed]** The two supernatural events, the gate opening and the summoning, get no visual build-up: a dark smudge, and a puddle of blue light.

### 7.9 Bubbles: shapes, placement and tails

- **[Observed] One bubble type throughout:** a white ellipse with a thin black outline. There are no variants for shouting, whispering, thoughts, phones or broadcasts. Off-panel phone voices (the office in chapter 3, Grandma's call in chapter 7) and the TV interview use the same ellipse, sometimes without a tail.
- **[Observed] Placement.**
  - Chapters 1–2: bubbles sit in empty headroom at the top of each image, so the art always leaves blank space above the characters.
  - Chapters 3–7: bubbles are mostly stacked in the white bands between panels, with long, thin straight tails that cross panel borders. A minority overlap the art. Who is speaking depends on left/right placement.
- **[Observed] Example of an attribution failure.** On chapter 6 page 20, "I'LL ASK WHAT PLACES THEY'RE SURVEYING NEXT." has a tail that ends in background between Nib and Taeo. The next bubble's tail runs off-panel to Taeo's cropped-off head.
- **[Judgement]** The bubbles are laid out like captions under photographs, not designed into the page.

### 7.10 Fonts

- **[Observed] Dialogue.** One all-caps comic-style typeface at one size for every character and emotion, with no bold for emphasis and no size change for shouting.
- **[Observed] System and UI text.** System windows, phone screens, the TV caption, chapter headers and end cards all use a plain sans. The system boxes are navy rounded rectangles with a cyan outline.
- **[Judgement]** Readability is good; personality is absent.

### 7.11 Sound effects (SFX)

- **[Measured] Only eight SFX in about 355 panels:**
  - chapter 1: "DING. DING.", "THUNK.", "VMMMM.";
  - chapter 2: "TIK.", "BRR.";
  - chapter 5: "THUP. THUP.";
  - chapter 6: "KRRR.";
  - none in chapters 3, 4 or 7.
- **[Observed] How they are rendered.** All are small typed words in navy with a white outline, each ending in a full stop. None is drawn, scaled or worked into the image. "THUP. THUP." sits in the gutter, away from the falling bags.

### 7.12 Chapter length and pacing

- **[Measured]** Panels per chapter fall from 64 / 61 / 62 to 55, 38, 41 and 34. Narration runs 3:13 to 5:49 per chapter.
- **[Judgement]**
  - Panel space goes to repeated wide shots and stacked dialogue rather than to story beats.
  - The scroll rhythm never changes: a same-size panel, a white band of one to four bubbles, another same-size panel. Nothing speeds up or slows down for emphasis.
  - The quiet chapters (2, 5, 7) would gain from wordless pauses and close-ups.
  - The key beats need size: a tall reveal panel for Nib's arrival and a full-width bleed for the gate.

### 7.13 What makes it look AI-generated (specific signs on the pages)

1. **Panel-to-panel design drift.** Nib's scale, eyes, belt colour, knife/thermos and skin colour; Taeo's footwear; relative heights (7.4). This is typical of panels generated one at a time.
2. **Set and effect drift.** Taeo's flat is redesigned twice, and the gate effect and the DRAW interface change between consecutive panels.
3. **Uniform photographic rendering.** Every panel has the same detail density and an eye-level, stock-photo composition, with near-identical camera set-ups repeated (chapters 2 and 7).
4. **Two visibly different layers.** Drawn text is missing or smeared (blank packaging and name tags) while the overlaid UI is crisp, and one phone screen is half blank (`ch/001/r2-p047`).
5. **Lettering added after the art.** Identical ellipses are parked in empty headroom (chapters 1–2) or in white bands (chapters 3–7), with long tails crossing borders. No bubble or SFX design is integrated into the drawing.
6. **SFX as typed words with full stops.**
7. **No artist's shorthand.** No speed lines, screentone, chibi or exaggerated faces, varied line weight, or deliberate simplification.
8. **A visible style break** between chapters 1–2 and 3–7.

### 7.14 Against professional Korean webtoons [Convention, unverified: general craft practice, not sourced in this session]

- **Panel variety.** Professional series vary panel width and height to set the tempo, use full-bleed and border-breaking panels for reveals, and cut between camera angles. Here almost every panel is a same-size, eye-level rectangle.
- **Lettering.** Professional lettering is integrated with the art: bubbles overlap panel edges and link panels, distinct shapes mark shouting, whispers, thoughts and phones, and hand-drawn SFX are sized and angled as part of the picture.
- **Hooks.** Professional chapters usually end on a hook (a reveal, a threat, a question). This series does so clearly once in seven chapters.
- **Production control.** Professional teams keep character design sheets and colour keys, so deviations like those in 7.4 would normally be caught before release.
- **Supernatural beats.** Professional action-fantasy webtoons give supernatural moments a visual build-up (lighting, particles, impact frames). The gate and the summoning here are visually flat.
- **Where this series holds up.** Clean, readable staging; good backgrounds; restrained, natural dialogue; and a coherent quiet tone.
