## 1. Episode format norms

**Cuts per episode (컷 수, cut count). Sources disagree, so here is who says what:**
- [D 2] A weekly Korean webtoon averages **70 cuts per episode**. This comes from a Hanyang Univ. Hospital study of 320 full-time creators plus 15 interviews, reported 2023-01-05. The same article quotes a 2020 KOCCA (Korea Creative Content Agency) survey at 45–50 cuts per week that "rose about 30% in two years". Creators work 9.9 h/day, and 11.8 h the day before a deadline.
- [D 3] Creators consider **53.9 cuts** appropriate, but the actual average is **64.6**. 45.4% say adjusting the cut count is hard. The article puts this near the 2023 KOCCA creator survey but does not explicitly attribute the figures to it, so treat the attribution as implied.
- [D 4] 2018 column: Korean artists average **60–90 cuts per week**, all in colour. Named weekly series run 60–90 (신의 탑 70–90, 아쿠아맨 75–90). A monthly series ran 220–250. Platform submission conditions require "1화당 60컷 이상" (at least 60 cuts per episode); the platform is not named. Japanese weekly manga is about 16 pages.
- [D 1] Kakao: where a contract states a minimum, it dropped from **60 to 50 cuts** from 2023-02-01. Kakao says it never enforced such clauses.
- [D 5, 3] The 2024 revised standard contract has the parties agree a contracted cut count and upper/lower volume limits, with extra pay for cuts beyond the contracted count.
- [D 8] Public contest (SBA Seoul Webtoon Contest 2020): "roughly 60–80" cuts per episode. It also asked for 2 finished episodes and a story able to run 40+ episodes.
- [D 10] Naver's 2018 new-artist contest asked for at least 20 cuts. This is a contest minimum, not a serial norm.
- [A 37] Early episodes ran 50–60 panels, now over 80, some over 100. The paper cites another source for this.
- [D 12] WEBTOON CANVAS staff (global English platform): an average WEBTOON Original has **30–60 panels**. [P 19] says about 60 for a typical weekly.
- [I] Korean commercial weekly series cluster at 60–80 cuts. 50 is the lowest contract floor any source states. 30–60 is the English-language Originals range. 80–100+ appears in action-heavy paid-wait titles.

**Scroll length.**
- No platform publishes an "episode height".
- Width specs:
  - Naver: 690 px, ≤5 MB per file, 50 MB total, JPG/GIF. The page is dated 2019 and edited 2022, so it may be outdated [D 21].
  - WEBTOON: 800 × 1,280 per image, auto-sliced, 20 MB total [D 12].
  - SBA contest: 1,280 px recommended, slices ≤7,999 px [D 8].
- Creator figures:
  - 800 × 30,000 px "fits a full episode" (panel count not given) [P 18].
  - A 70-panel chapter used seven 20,000 px files at 1,600 px wide, which is about 70,000 px at 800 px [P 19].
  - About 2 panels per 1,280 px [P 16]. Another creator sets canvas height as panel count × 1,280 px [P 22].
- [I] That implies about 640–1,280 px per cut at 800 px width. So 60–70 cuts is roughly 38,000–90,000 px, about 30–70 phone screens. The band is wide; measure our own reference episodes.

**Reading time.** I found no measured per-episode reading time for webtoons. Web novels are built for 5–15 minute mobile sessions [A 40, 45]. A book blurb claims about 2 minutes per web-novel episode [V 54]. Do not state a webtoon figure.

**Weekly release norms.**
- Weekly ("매주 1회 연재") is the baseline [D 2]. Twice-weekly authors exist [D 6].
- 44.4% of readers follow each title's release schedule, and 21.7% wait and binge [D 7].
- Hiatus (휴재) rules:
  - Kakao: 2 per 40 episodes [D 1].
  - Naver: 1 per 25 episodes. Weekly authors get up to 2 per year, twice-weekly up to 4 [D 6].
  - Ministry standard contract: 2 per 50 episodes [D 5].
- Production week: 2 days for story and 콘티 (storyboard), 3–4 days for sketch and colour, about 1 day off [D 4]. Cho Seok's 2016 week was Monday 콘티, Tuesday half the art, Wednesday deliver [P 27].
- Kakao's paid-wait model: Solo Leveling's first 3 chapters were free; the wait period is not specified [A 37].

**How an episode is planned (콘티).**
- The 콘티 is "the script of a comic". Its main job is controlling the flow of information, and unnecessary parts must be removed. Pacing comes from the number, size and ratio of cuts, the amount of text (more text feels slower), balloon spacing, and in/out transitions. It must be readable by others [P 24].
- Text-first 글콘티 (text storyboard): numbered cuts ("#1. 주인공이 들어온다."), with dialogue, SFX and narration separated. One creator's tool totals cuts and dialogue volume, which exposed over-reliance on monologue [P 25].
- Thumbnails first, with dialogue typed into the thumbnails. Outline the highlights instead of scripting everything. Each episode starts in one place and ends in another. Preview in phone view [P 18, 19, 21].
- At pitch, Naver's 2019 call wanted a proposal, a finished ep. 1, and 콘티 for 3–5 episodes [D 9]. A book blurb says platforms usually want a planning document plus the first 1–3 finished episodes [V 11].

**Web-novel chapters per webtoon episode.**
- **No source gives a ratio.** I did not find one.
- What the sources do say: a faithful 1:1 transfer fails, and text and information must be cut [P 46]. PDs check how a story converts into scenes and whether events can be condensed or expanded [P 49].
- Raw inputs for our own count: web-novel instalments run about 4,000–6,000 characters [V 59] (5,000 minimum per [V 54]). 전지적 독자 시점 (Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint) has 551 novel chapters [A 45].

## 2. Vertical-scroll direction (세로 스크롤 연출)

**White space (여백, 컷 간격, gutter).**
- Numbers (800 px canvas):
  - ≥200 px between panels.
  - 600–1,000 px between scenes or locations to signal time or distance [D 12; P 13].
  - Another creator: ≥250 px, and about 1,000 px for time changes [P 14].
  - Content Curve uses a 1,600 px canvas: small gutter 400 px, standard 800 px, transition 1,600+ px [P 19]. [I] Halved for 800 px that is 200 / 400 / 800+, which matches the platform numbers.
- What gutters do:
  - Narrow gaps speed action; wide gaps add suspense and slow the scene [P 15].
  - More panels between beats feels slower, and a larger distance means more scrolling [P 17].
  - Gap size creates scene transitions and psychological effect [P 21].
  - Leave room for bubbles so they do not cover characters [P 17].
  - "Most readers are put off when panels are too close together" [P 16].
- [A 38, abstract only] The infinite canvas lets the reader control pacing. Blank space, panel rhythm, close-ups and information reveals shape suspense.
- Many early scroll artists dropped panel borders and put bubbles and narration outside panels [A 32].

**Panel width and height.**
- Show no more than 2 panels on a phone screen [D 12; P 14]. Ideally 1 per "page" [P 15], about 2 per 1,280 px [P 16].
- Vary width, shape and size to signal importance [D 12; P 13]. Uniform rectangles read as calm or monotonous; skewed, sharp shapes read as sudden action [P 13]. Angled frames are dynamic, square frames calm [P 15].
- Avoid long horizontal panels, which force phone rotation [P 17]. Early action webtoons rotated wide cuts 90° [A 31]. A critic praises tilting wide scenes diagonally to bridge horizontal and vertical [V 28].
- Keep a line and its speaker on one screen [P 19]. Keep screen direction consistent within a scene [P 15].

**Close-ups vs wide shots.**
- Wide shots establish context. Close-ups show emotion at key moments. Extra panels stretch an action's timing [P 20].
- Sports-webtoon study: close-ups, background-less scenes and object shots drive transitions and psychological immersion. Saturation and brightness contrast emphasises key elements [A 36, abstract only].
- Shot-based direction borrowed from animation: character-composition shots and time shots [A 34].
- The Solo Leveling art team puts character acting first, with background, staging and motion supporting it [P 30].
- Backgrounds are often simplified for small screens [P 20, 21].

**Impact panels (임팩트 컷).** Full-screen panels are allowed and encouraged [P 13]. Size signals significance [D 12]. Impact panels get full effort (fights, big romantic reveals) while normal panels get 60–70% of effort [P 19].

**Effect lines (효과선).**
- **No numeric rule found.**
- Sound effects sit on the object that makes the sound and may break the panel border [P 20].
- Motion blur signals action; blur focuses the main character [P 18].
- Aligning scroll direction with the action's direction gives a sense of motion [A 31].
- A course lists 효과선/집중선 as a topic but gives no rules [V 26].

**Speech bubbles (말풍선): placement and reading order.**
- A working artist's critique: the eye moves in a Z. Alternate bubble weight left/right between scenes. Put two bubbles upper-left and lower-right. Put bubbles in the gutter space rather than cramming them over expressions [P 23].
- Other sources:
  - Left to right, then down [P 20].
  - Order bubbles left to right so the reader knows who speaks first [P 15].
  - Follow reading order, do not wrap around drawings, do not overuse zigzag [P 24].
  - Zigzag layouts guide the S/Z eye path without borders [A 33].
  - Place bubbles top-left or top-right and add tails last [P 18].
  - Reserve bubble space at sketch stage [P 19, 20].
- Lettering: round bubbles for normal speech, jagged for shouting, anger or surprise, wavy for fear; bold font for strong emotion, italic for nervousness [P 16]. Webtoon text is larger than print [P 17]; typical 12–30 px on an 800 px canvas [D 12].

**Dialogue per panel.**
- ≤3 bubbles per panel, counting thought bubbles and narration boxes [D 12; P 13, 14].
- A bubble is normally 2 lines, "never more than 3" [P 23].
- Creators cap a bubble at about 2 sentences [P 18] or 4 sentences [P 17].
- A vendor page suggests about 35 words per panel (translated page; treat loosely) [V 20].

**Narration (내레이션) and inner monologue (독백).**
- Frequent narration can break scene immersion. Kangfull's character-viewpoint monologue built intimacy, but he used it less later [A 33].
- One creator's cut-counting tool revealed over-reliance on monologue, which led to giving other characters more screen time [P 25].
- Narration boxes count toward the 3-bubble cap [D 12]. More text means slower pacing [P 24]. When adapting novels, cut text and information [P 46].
- Positive example: a necromancer webtoon praised for inner-voice narration with self-deprecating humour, and a closing narration line [V 64].

**Reaction panels (리액션 컷) and SD/chibi gag panels (SD 개그 컷).**
- "Reaction" is the third beat of an action sequence [P 17, 20].
- **I found no credible source on SD gag panels.** Related material: an author cameo used to ease tension and add humour [A 33]. Korea-specific gag codes can break immersion for global readers, and one studio limits them from the planning stage [V 49]. Any SD rule would be my inference.

**Action pacing.**
- Three beats: anticipation, action, reaction or outcome. More panels between beats means slower [P 17, 20]. Narrow gutters speed it up [P 15].
- Arc-level pacing: Solo Leveling typically defeats an enemy within 3–4 episodes, and quick battle resolution is described as a core engagement strategy [A 37].
- The art team describes action as dense, with fantasy-style staging [P 30].

## 3. Episode structure and retention

**Opening.**
- A Tappytoon PD checks whether the protagonist's emotion and goal are clear early, whether per-episode hook points can be designed, and whether there are visually iconic scenes or characters [P 49].
- A Kakao PD (하유림): "지금은 1회 초반, 정말 몇 번의 스크롤에 캐릭터의 매력과 관계성을 보여줘야 하는 문법이 강해졌다." (Now the grammar demands showing character appeal and relationships within the first few scrolls of ep. 1) [P 47].
- Solo Leveling ch. 1–3: the first panel states identity and setting, ch. 2 explains the hunter system, ch. 3 ends with the hero in danger [A 37].
- "이해가능성" (intelligibility) is the core of early immersion. Over-detailed worldbuilding makes readers "study" [V 53]. Webtoon readers leave when text or content is too heavy to follow [P 46].

**Endings (절단신공, the cliffhanger ending).**
- Solo Leveling ends episodes by opening a new problem instead of closing the conflict. The paper calls this 승전결기 (development, climax, resolution, then a new introduction). Tower of God usually ends on resolution or reflection [A 37].
- Thriller webtoons use repeated cliffhangers and delayed information, and paid-unlock models tie to end-of-episode cliffhangers [A 38, abstract only].
- Creator advice: end on "a small cliffhanger or an intriguing moment" whenever possible [P 18].
- Counterpoint from a PD (김은서): each episode needs a minimal sense of completion, and "억지로 끊는 게 아니라, 서사와 감정의 흐름 안에서 이어져야 한다" (not a forced cut; it must follow the flow of story and emotion) [P 48].
- Web novels: maximise curiosity at the last free episode before the paywall [V 55].
- So "cliffhanger every episode" is partly right. Paid-wait titles do it, but a senior PD warns against forced ones.

**사이다 vs 고구마.**
- 사이다 is the satisfying payoff (lit. "soda"). 고구마 is frustrating build-up (lit. "sweet potato" that chokes).
- Mechanism: repeated 결핍생성–욕망충족 (lack creation, then desire fulfilment) [V 56]. Retribution against antagonists is the 사이다 pleasure [A 44].
- A slow, frustrating build-up is "치명적" (fatal) [V 54]. Kakao PD: tolerance for the "고구마 구간" has dropped a lot [P 47].
- A web novelist's advice: do not show the protagonist being harmed for long; crises should be overcome quickly [A 43]. Munchkin stories run on a short per-chapter crisis–resolution–crisis loop [A 42].
- **No measured frequency of 사이다 moments exists in what I found.** Proxies:
  - 3+ boring episodes in a row is serious; 2+ episodes without the protagonist is risky [V 54].
  - Solo Leveling resolves an enemy in 3–4 episodes [A 37].
  - Regression novels put a partial catharsis inside the roughly 25 free episodes [A 39].
- Nuance: one lower-intensity episode after a big event is acceptable ("not every episode needs a major event") [P 16].

**First 1–3 episodes.**
- "초반 3화" (the first 3 episodes) is industry folk wisdom. Munpia's free window is roughly ep. 25–50, and failing to immerse readers there is called a flop [V 53].
- 산경, author of 재벌집 막내아들, says to keep readers through "최소한 5화까지" (at least ep. 5) and pour the best material into 1–5 [V 55].
- A study of four regression/reincarnation novels found the free episodes skip the slow setup stages and include a taste of the "hero returns with power" stage, so readers feel partial catharsis before paying [A 39].
- Solo Leveling's free chapters establish protagonist, job and dungeon quickly [A 37].
- Kakao PDs want early character appeal; Tappytoon wants "초반 회차의 스토리 안정성" (story stability in early episodes) [P 47, 49].

**Common pacing mistakes named by sources.**
- Slow build-up [P 47; V 54].
- Setup-heavy openings [A 39; V 53].
- Text and information overload [P 46].
- Over-detailed worldbuilding [V 53].
- Protagonist absent for 2+ episodes [V 54].
- Forced cliffhangers [P 48].
- Childish dialogue [V 55, 59].
- Slow, character-focused arcs hurt a paid-wait model (Tower of God's 12-episode arc) [A 37].

## 4. Genre conventions: hunter / system / gate and summoner / necromancer

**Hunter world kit (헌터물, 게이트물, 시스템물).**
- Gates open and monsters come through. Dungeons must be cleared. Hunters awaken (각성) abilities. Gates are graded [60, 62, 70].
- Ranks:
  - Solo Leveling uses E to S [60, 70].
  - 공무원 헌터의 S급 퇴사일지 uses F / A / S / EX [62].
  - Other titles name D and B ranks [61] and E/D grades [64].
  - There is no universal ladder. Pick one and fix it.
- A rank is fixed at awakening ("한 번 각성한 등급은 변하지 않는") and there is a re-assessment mechanism. Solo Leveling's hero is the exception [68, 70].
- Institutions: a hunter association or management bureau tests, regulates and grows suspicious [61, 62]. Guilds recruit and raid [61]. Magic stones and loot [37]. A "double dungeon" is the inciting event in Solo Leveling [60, 69].

**Core reader promises.**
- Game elements quantify goals, rules and feedback, giving clarity and motivation [A 42]. Growth is trackable "at a glance" and explains why he keeps getting stronger [V 60]. The reviewer adds the balance must be tight and the process "must not be easy" [V 60].
- Vicarious satisfaction [A 40, 42].
- Second chance: a Kakao webnovel manager's reading of 회빙환 is "one more chance, and confidence you will not fail" [V 50].
- Readers want to be special: "우리는 특별하고 싶어 한다" [V 57].
- Ordinary protagonists beating extraordinary ones [A 43].

**Protagonist types.**
- 성장형 (growth-type) underdog: the weakest E-rank becomes strongest [60, 68].
- 서포터 / low-class awakener becoming a 먼치킨 (overpowered protagonist) [61].
- Cursed or rare class [64].
- Reluctant ordinary worker thrust into power [62].
- Returning legend or 고인물 (veteran who dominates), 회춘물 (rejuvenation story) [63].
- 회귀자 (regressor) with foreknowledge as a "cheat key" [A 44].
- Hidden strength or class: Solo Leveling's hidden E-rank [68]; 웨폰 크리에이터's hero plans to hide his strength for two years [61].
- Munchkin protagonists in this genre are expected to stay sincere and ethical [A 42].

**What the status window (상태창) is used for.**
- Only the protagonist can see it. It replaces learning the world through other characters, and numbers stand in for descriptions of enemy strength [A 41].
- Quests set goals, rewards and penalties. They mark the events that make up each episode and are often linear. Penalties are often severe, such as death [A 41].
- It makes growth visible [V 60] and works as a turning-point reveal. Examples: a Player message [61], "갑자기 상태창이 뜨게 됩니다" (a status window suddenly appears) [63], the status window revealing a cursed class and E-grade [64].
- It serves a wish for certainty and trust in numbers [V 57]. Daily quests act as gamified discipline [A 42].

**회빙환 (regression / possession / reincarnation).**
- Master motifs of the genre [A 41]. A time-based story model driven by regret and improvement [A 40].
- Typical structure: a hard-working protagonist, an unjust end, a second chance, then the reader expects the chance to be seized [V 50].
- Critics say it is now so fixed that it is barely a genre [V 51]. One columnist argues it suits older readers [V 52].
- Regression is optional for a hunter story [61, 62, 63 do not need it].

**Summoner / necromancer / tamer (소환사물, 네크로맨서, 테이머물).**
- Solo Leveling's hero becomes the Shadow Monarch, a necromancer. He extracts shadows from defeated enemies, shadows grow, and only commander-grade shadows can speak [70].
- Summons are treated as companions rather than tools of exploitation [A 42].
- 나는 스켈레톤을 키운다 (I Raise Skeletons) [64]:
  - A cursed necromancer who can only summon skeletons; the class is extremely rare.
  - A status window reveals the cursed class and E-grade, then a hidden condition gives a D-grade upgrade and an "awakened skeleton".
  - Praised for cute skeleton nicknames, inner-voice narration and the unfairness of a luck-based class system as an emotional hook.
- 나 혼자 네크로맨서 [65, 66]:
  - Monsters pour onto a campus in ch. 1.
  - The hero gets the necromancer class, kills goblins with a chair, then revives them as undead.
  - A reviewer calls the plot near-cliché but the pacing smooth and brisk.
  - It is billed as a 먼치킨 story with large-scale battles.
- 금발의 정령사 (Blonde Spirit Summoner) [67]: a reincarnated heroine who wants an easy life and chooses spirit summoning.
- **No analytic source on 테이머물 (tamer stories) was found.**

**First-arc beats. [I] I assembled these from five reviewer-described openings: 60, 61, 62, 63, 64.**
1. A low-status hero in a game-like world doing small-time work.
2. A routine gate or dungeon outing turns lethal (double dungeon; hidden passage wipes the guild; trapped with a boss).
3. Near-death moment or wish for another chance.
4. System or status window appears as the turning point.
5. First exploitation of the power: a boss kill, a rank-up, or a first minion.
6. Institutions notice the hero and he conceals his strength.
7. A larger mystery hook (unidentified gates, hidden mastermind, a coming Player selection).

## 5. Dialogue style

- **Line length.** See section 2: ≤2–3 lines per bubble [P 23], ≤3 bubbles per panel [D 12]. On phones, web novelists use one sentence or one line of dialogue per paragraph [V 59].
- **Exchange shape.**
  - Write the dialogue first, "탁구공을 주고받듯" (like table tennis). Tension-building line types are persuasion, requests, threats, mockery and sarcasm. Every line needs a purpose ("엿듣고 싶은 대화" is the target: conversation worth overhearing). Drop subjects and particles as real speech does, and give each character a speech style [V 58]. That source is an AI summary of a creator's YouTube lecture whose credentials are not shown.
  - **Interruptions: no source found.**
  - **Villain taunts: no source beyond the "mockery / sarcasm" tension types in [V 58].** Solo Leveling ch. 27 ends on a new character's menacing line [A 37].
- **Bystander commentary as exposition.** A supporting character marvelling at the protagonist's looks or abilities delivers explanation naturally [V 58].
- **Exposition without lectures.**
  - Use the system window [A 41].
  - Use an object or action instead of speech. In 내가 키운 S급들 (I Raised the S-Rankers), the dying character hands over a gate stone instead of explaining monsters, potion and gate [P 46].
  - Solo Leveling states identity in the first panel and explains the hunter system in ch. 2 [A 37].
  - **No source for the "knowledgeable side character" device.**
- **Narration vs dialogue balance.** See section 2. Frequent narration disrupts immersion [A 33], and monologue overuse is measurable [P 25]. One web novelist was told by an editor to increase dialogue volume [V 58].
- **Tone.**
  - Web-novel dialogue that reads as childish is a known failure [V 55, 59].
  - "Short" is not the same as "clipped". The sources stress intelligibility as the core of early immersion [V 53]. [I] The failure to avoid is lines that drop the subject or object so a reader cannot tell who is doing what.

## Rules we should adopt

Format and planning
1. Plan weekly episodes at 55–70 cuts. Flag under 50 as short and over 80 as peak-action or finale only. (D 1,2,3,4,8; A 37; I)
2. Never show more than 2 panels on one phone screen (check at 800 × 1,280). (D 12; P 14)
3. Build at 800 px wide (690 px if targeting Naver KR) and export slices ≤1,280 px. Record our own px per cut and episode height from reference episodes before locking a template. (D 12, 21; I)
4. Write the 콘티 first: numbered cuts, with dialogue, narration and SFX tagged separately. Count cuts, bubbles and monologue lines before final art. (P 24, 25)
5. Cut any panel that carries no new information. A conti must be readable by a stranger. (P 24)
6. Have ep. 1 finished and conti for eps. 2–5 before anyone reviews the series. (D 9; V 11)

Vertical-scroll direction
7. Gutters at 800 px width: ≥200 px between consecutive beats, about 400 px standard, 600–1,000 px at scene or time change. (D 12; P 13, 14; I for the 400)
8. Tighten gutters in fast exchanges and action. Lengthen them before a reveal. (P 15, 17, 21)
9. Panel size follows importance. The largest or longest panel in an episode is its peak beat. Reserve full-screen impact panels for 1–3 beats (the count is my inference) and spend full effort there, about 60–70% on routine panels. (D 12; P 13, 19; I)
10. Open each scene with a wide or medium establishing panel. Hold close-ups for emotional peaks. (P 20; A 36)
11. Stage action in three beats (anticipation, action, reaction or outcome). Add panels only where time should slow. (P 17, 20)
12. Run the dominant motion down the strip. Avoid long horizontal panels; tilt them diagonally or crop tall. (A 31; P 17; V 28)
13. Keep screen direction constant within a scene. Keep a line and its speaker on one screen. (P 15, 19)
14. Preview every page in phone-proportion view. Each screen needs one focal point. (P 16, 21)

Bubbles and text
15. ≤3 bubbles per panel, counting thought bubbles and narration boxes. (D 12; P 13, 14)
16. ≤2 lines per bubble (3 absolute max) and ≤2 sentences. Split longer speech into linked bubbles. (P 23, 18, 17)
17. Put bubbles in the gutter or negative space, never over faces or hands. Read order is top-left to bottom-right. Alternate left/right weight between scenes. Reserve bubble space at thumbnail stage and type dialogue into the thumbnails. (P 23, 24, 15, 19, 20)
18. Letter large and simple. Round bubbles for normal speech, jagged for shouting, wavy for fear. Bold for strong emotion, italic for nervous. Test on a phone. (P 16, 17; D 12)
19. Keep monologue and narration lines fewer than dialogue lines in every episode. Each box must add what the image cannot. Review any run of 3+ narration-only panels. (A 33; P 24, 25; I for the thresholds)

Dialogue and exposition
20. Every line must do something (persuade, threaten, mock, bargain, reveal). Delete lines that only restate the image. (V 58, 55)
21. Short must stay clear. Each line names or implies its subject and object. Test: have a cold reader read the dialogue without the art. (V 53; I)
22. Deliver exposition through the system window, a bystander's reaction, or a knowledgeable character. Never put two explanation-only bubbles in a row. (A 41; V 58; I for the "two")
23. When converting text to panels, replace explanation with an action or object. (P 46)
24. No childish lines. Give each character a distinct speech register. (V 55, 58, 59)

Retention
25. Ep. 1: within the first few scrolls (about 3–5 screens, my reading of "a few scrolls") show the protagonist's appeal or lack and one hook. No lore dump. (P 47, 49; I)
26. Eps. 1–3 establish protagonist, rank or job and setting, deliver the inciting disaster, and end ep. 3 in danger or with a first taste of power. (A 37, 39; V 53)
27. Put the best material in eps. 1–5. No pure setup episodes. (V 55, 53)
28. Each episode starts in one situation, ends in another, and feels complete. (P 18, 48)
29. The last 1–3 cuts open a new question, threat or choice that grows from the scene. Never cut arbitrarily. (A 37; P 48)
30. Pay off (win, power-up, reveal, antagonist humbled) at least once every about 3 episodes. Never 3 build-up episodes in a row. One low-intensity episode after a major event is fine. (V 54; A 37; P 16; I)
31. Keep setbacks short and keep the protagonist on screen. (A 43; V 54; P 47)

Genre
32. Pick one rank ladder (E/F up to S) and never change it. Gates are graded, ranks are fixed at awakening unless the protagonist is the exception, an association tests and regulates, and guilds recruit. (V 68, 70, 61, 62)
33. Protagonist: low rank or odd class, plus a private edge (system, hidden class or foreknowledge), plus a sincere ethic, with visible growth steps. (V 60, 61, 64; A 42, 43)
34. Status window: first appears at the turning point after near-death. Minimal fields (level, rank, class, quest with reward and penalty). Updated only at rank-ups or key gains. (A 41, 42; V 60, 63, 64)
35. Summoner or necromancer: summons are named companions with personalities. Summoning or extraction is the growth engine. Rarity or a curse is the underdog hook. (A 42; V 64, 65, 70)
36. Build episodes 1–10 on the 7-beat first-arc map in section 4. (I, from V 60–64)
