## 1. What is fixed, what is not

### Established canon and constraints
- Modern life coexists with portal dungeons. Clearing the boss closes that dungeon's gate.
- Taeo is a young, cautious employed orphan in a small apartment. His younger sibling lives with their grandparents. He helps financially and has ordinary obligations.
- His reluctance to enter dungeons lasts. Companions go inside; remote leadership is the core premise, not a timid starting state that must be cured by sword fighting.
- His roster grows slowly through gacha. Nib is first: a weak, adult goblin scout, neither a mascot nor a hidden god.
- Ordinary summons have limited intelligence and are generally nonverbal. Taeo's exceptions are sentient, speaking individuals.
- Creature and hero bonds matter. Permanent fusion produces one individual with a blended personality, not a temporary transformation or two alternating voices.
- Taeo eventually builds a guild/empire and becomes unmatched around chapter 200 through gradual, earned development.
- The approved opening remains slow. Chapter 1 ends on Nib's first words. Chapter 2 covers the first meeting, temporary shelter, and only a tiny increase in trust.

### Verified script facts, not retroactive foreshadowing claims
Chapter 1 establishes the grocery job provisionally, everyday family contact, a simple-command porter, a nearby gate incident, awakening, a saved assessment contact, and one draw. Chapter 2 establishes fear on both sides, an unobstructed exit, an unsuccessful novice attempt to reopen the interface, and Nib's wish to be sent back. Neither chapter establishes summon mortality, dismissal, costs, control, telepathy, resurrection, origin worlds, fusion, or how the interface works. The shared cup and open exit are already meaningful human actions; they were not secretly planted evidence of future mechanics.

### All additions below are proposals
Names other than Taeo and Nib, ranks, factions, family details, idol history, specific powers, deaths, origin explanations, and chapter outcomes are proposed. Do not quietly introduce them into Chapter 2. Before a later script uses a proposed rule, promote that rule into the live canon sheet and check its downstream dependencies.

### Recommended decisions, rather than a questionnaire
1. Keep Nib unfused throughout the main run. He remains an equal founding partner and useful scout, not a body sacrificed to make a prettier hero. Show fusion through two other developed companions.
2. Make Taeo's idol genuinely admirable and imperfect. Their eventual disagreement concerns what safety costs, not a reveal that every good deed was fake.
3. Make the ordinary summons actual magical constructs, while Taeo's companions are transported people. Do not invalidate every ordinary summoner's ethical life with a surprise universal enslavement twist.
4. Let the central mystery resolve by roughly chapter 175. The climax is what the characters choose to do with the answer, not the last-minute arrival of a stronger hidden god.
5. Let the final victory give companions a real right to leave. Taeo's power fantasy culminates in people choosing him when they no longer have to.

## 2. The dramatic engine

**Visible promise:** a frightened grocery worker sends his increasingly extraordinary companions where he cannot go, eventually commanding the strongest force in the world.

**Emotional question:** can someone who needs others to take the danger for him build a relationship that is not ownership?

**Taeo's active contradiction:** he is careful with other people's immediate safety, yet his fear and obligation to support his family can make him justify decisions other people must live with. His positive ambition is to invent a remote-clear method that earns enough to own a permanent base and prove he can excel without entering a dungeon. He likes the prospect of being good at something rare, having money, and setting a record. He can pursue an attractive target too aggressively and resent being treated only as a careful helper. His flaw is not generic low self-esteem. He confuses assuming responsibility with retaining final control. His growth is learning to share authority while remaining accountable.

**Nib's active contradiction:** he wants autonomy and a way home, but distrust makes it hard to accept help or admit attachment. His survival skills are practical rather than supernatural. He eventually chooses leadership, which requires trusting people who are less good at hiding than he is.

**Main causal chain:** Taeo needs income and answers → chooses a paid, supervised trial instead of selling access to Nib → a successful rescue creates a reputation for safe extraction → that reputation attracts people expensive guilds overlook → protecting those people requires organization → organization gives Taeo political and logistical power → its success makes the old gate economy and source-world emergency network unable to ignore him → he can finally win any fight but must decide what his victory obligates everyone else to do.

The story should alternate domestic intimacy, exploration, competition, rescue, negotiation, investigation, and war. No arc exists solely to level up. Each has a local problem that genuinely ends, an irreversible choice, and a changed relationship.

## 3. A world already in motion

### Six pre-existing S-rank public figures
All six have recognizable careers, allies, failures, advertising/public images, and ongoing goals before Taeo awakens. They are introduced by relevance rather than a ranked-character slideshow. S-rank is an institutional capacity/risk designation; it does not imply a reliable one-dimensional win order.

**Han Seoryun, 'The Returning Captain' — Taeo's proposed idol.** A veteran Korean barrier specialist and rescue commander. Her famous footage is not a perfect kill: she hands her shield to an injured porter and walks the last person through an exit before turning back to cover it. When Taeo was younger, after his parents' deaths for an unrelated, still-undecided reason, he watched that broadcast beside his sibling. He remembers the relieved families on the pavement. She represented the possibility that dangerous work could end with someone coming home. Do not make her secretly responsible for his orphanhood. She has lost people; 'brings her team home' is an earned reputation, not a magically spotless record. Her project is a national rescue network independent of guild bidding. Her flaw: she will personally absorb unreasonable burdens and can mistake controlled access for protection. Taeo first owns an old inexpensive team patch, not an altar of merchandise. She is his idol before she knows his name.

**Seo Jiwon, 'Sunline' — public celebrity with a real occupation.** A radiant spear hunter whose advertisements and charity appearances irritate people who think fame means unseriousness. She wants the first officially recorded solo clear of an aerial-class dungeon and takes her public image seriously as an aesthetic project. She spends conspicuously on spectacular equipment and likes being admired. A long-term hospital sponsorship is one real obligation, not her entire identity or a certificate of sainthood. Brilliant at decisive offense, weaker in prolonged confined-space operations. Her dispute with Taeo concerns cameras and disclosure: evidence can protect his companions, but public visibility can also consume them. She helps once without recruiting him and later refuses a request that would destroy her hospital funding. Neither choice makes her fake.

**Park Doyun, 'The Cartographer' — elite summoner foil.** Commands several sophisticated but nonverbal constructs through immense practiced attention. He has spent years mapping efficient evacuation routes. Not cruel, not jealous that Taeo owns a rarer unit. He initially thinks Taeo mistakes verbal mimicry for personhood, then changes his conclusion when the evidence changes. His immediate obsession is a perfect zero-waste construct routine, and he will cancel a prestigious appearance to fix a tiny inefficiency. He wants an ordinary summoner to beat his record using a method he taught. His wish to retire from public performances conflicts with that competitive pride. He remains uniquely useful because he can do work without asking living people to risk themselves. His quieter friendship with Nib crosses their initial disagreement.

**Mara Velez, 'Undertow' — international field authority.** A coastal rescue hunter whose fluid-control abilities excel in flooded gates and are unsuitable for some dry interiors. She is building multinational emergency access agreements after a rescue delay killed colleagues, but her immediate private goal is a year exploring an unmapped ocean trench. She is tired of being the reliable person everyone can summon to a committee. She will contest Taeo's private control of shared infrastructure, even while defending companions' rights. No jealousy plot or automatic romance.

**Ibrahim Sayegh, 'The Weightbearer' — builder rather than tournament opponent.** Uses gravity management to stabilize enormous structures and move supplies. His teams support cities around recurring gate zones. He is politically influential because places still function after he leaves. His priority is not ending all gates immediately: some regions rely on gate-energy equipment, and an abrupt shutdown would cripple hospitals. He makes the transition problem concrete without excusing perpetual exploitation.

**Viktor Orlov, 'Red Interval' — difficult ally, not a disguised demon.** A spectacular close-range hunter whose accelerated bursts have a real recovery interval. He led successful emergency interventions and believes centralized command saves more lives than negotiated consent. He seeks leadership of a multinational rapid-response force. He can win a confrontation with Taeo early, suffer a defeat later, and remain ideologically opposed without escalating into a murderer merely to justify the protagonist.

**Relationship hierarchy:** Seoryun, Doyun and Jiwon are the central recurring elites. Mara, Ibrahim and Viktor receive consequential visits and independent developments, not equal subplot quotas. Their disagreements must include ambition, taste, competitive pride and weariness, not six versions of the same safety debate.

### How they enter the story
- Chapters 3–6: one ordinary TV interview or an old patch introduces Seoryun; the scene must answer what Taeo admires, not recite her record. A current Sunline ad exists as background texture, not mandatory future payoff.
- Chapters 12–20: Doyun's publicly available training material helps Taeo and fails to explain Nib. His first personal appearance follows a professional reason.
- Chapters 29–40: Seoryun meets Taeo through a rescue result; she does not arrive because destiny told her to.
- Chapters 45–70: Jiwon and Doyun become active during a public event and its consequences.
- Chapters 85–110: Mara and Ibrahim matter to the geographic expansion; readers already know their names from relevant news.
- Chapters 120–145: Viktor's coalition changes the available choices. He has been pursuing it independently for years.

**Keep scale honest:** a famous S-rank might share a ten-minute conversation and then leave for another emergency. Their lives cannot freeze whenever Taeo is absent. At least once an S-rank solves a substantial offscreen crisis that directly improves the world without his help.

## 4. Recurring cast with jobs, wants, and the ability to say no

Names are provisional. Avoid introducing this entire list at once.

- **Taeo's younger sibling:** wants reliable time together and eventually a future not chosen solely to reduce Taeo's financial burden. No surprise top-rank awakening, repeated kidnapping, or reward for endorsing every sacrifice. A missed visit remains painful even after money stops being scarce. Later chooses a civilian path and asks Taeo to attend an ordinary milestone during an inconvenient but non-catastrophic week.
- **Grandparents:** value security but differ about the work. Grandmother asks direct questions; grandfather tends to accept Taeo's reassurances until one visible injury forces a harder conversation. Neither exists only to dispense wisdom or die for motivation.
- **Eunchae, grocery coworker:** wants to qualify for a food-business license and eventually run her own shop. Useful in the beginning because she knows Taeo's avoidance, not because she is secretly a hunter. She does not become his unpaid secretary. Later purchases from guild suppliers on fair terms and will reject a vanity rescue investment.
- **Yuri Chae, assessment officer:** competent public employee trying to build a workable category for an unprecedented case. She keeps ordinary applicants moving, does not expose Nib for a promotion, and cannot waive every rule. Her ambition is a regional safety post; supporting Taeo risks that career only at a specific contentious decision, not every time she appears.
- **Im Sejin, B-rank field medic:** wants a clinic with predictable hours after years of traveling contracts. Joins early operations on paid terms and insists that 'summoned' is not a medical diagnosis. Leaves active raids before the final act and remains an effective clinic director. Not a permanent power-up dispenser.
- **Ryu Minho, licensed young rival:** technically better trained, socially confident, and burdened by a small family guild's debts. Wants his crew to survive without becoming a subsidiary. He beats Taeo fairly in an early paid assessment; loses a later contract to Taeo's approach; chooses a third path as an independent coalition member. His resentment is sometimes justified when Taeo receives attention he did not earn alone.
- **Director Baek Hojin, regional guild executive:** believes consolidation makes rescue equipment affordable. His guild has genuinely saved towns. He attempts exclusivity clauses and market pressure; he has a board, costs, and reputational limits. He does not order assassinations over a rejected contract. Later splits with the actual violent antagonist rather than sharing every villain's agenda.

### Slowly growing companion roster
Target roughly 7–9 enduring individuals by chapter 120, not thirty collectible portraits. Guests and civilian immigrants do not count as Taeo's personal combat summons. Some chapters contain no summon system at all.

1. **Nib, adult goblin scout (chapter 1).** Weak in direct combat. Good at patience, remembering routes, small practical repairs, and recognizing when he does not know enough. Wants news of the people he left and ultimately control over where he lives. Learns modern literacy and safety conventions with visible mistakes. Later leads scouts who are physically stronger than he is. His advanced usefulness arises from experience and trustworthy judgment, not a buried divine bloodline. He can refuse a mission and be wrong about a person.
2. **Orrin, an adult tortoise-like stoneback carrier (roughly 21–25).** Sentient creature, dry humor, slow movements, strong spatial memory. Wants an inhabited home rather than another campaign. Can hold weight and provide cover, not instantly tank an S-rank boss. Enjoys a roof garden and dislikes being described as equipment. His final place is a civic one as much as a martial one.
3. **Sera, adult human archer from a river settlement (roughly 42–48).** Wants her younger adult brother's whereabouts resolved, but has a life beyond searching: competitive, musical, occasionally vain. Honest about enjoying skill and money. Rejects Taeo's effort to turn every mission into a rescue at their expense. Searches end with a definite answer by 110, rather than perpetual bait.
4. **Kesh, adult moth-like signal keeper (roughly 63–70).** Speaks, reads distant pulses, and loves crowded celebrations despite sensory limits. Wants to rebuild a lost communication craft with an apprentice. Low endurance and susceptible to noisy fields. A strong social character rather than a cute pet. Becomes a co-designer of remote operations, not an all-knowing radar.
5. **Dara, adult armored healer (roughly 83–88).** A disciplined field surgeon whose healing closes wounds but does not replace blood, erase exhaustion, or resurrect. Wants to stop being defined by the patients she could not save. Can be abrasive and funny, chooses a small number of close relationships. Learns local medicine from Sejin.
6. **Tal, adult ashwood guardian (roughly 99–104).** A mobile plant-bodied person with a deliberate manner, selfish enjoyment of new places, and an unfinished promise to a vanished community. Shields by redistributing damage into his own structure. Neither a pure saint nor simply 'nature speaks.' Strong friendship and principled disagreements with Dara precede their fusion question.
7. **Meret, adult duelist/historian (roughly 133–140).** Once served a source-world border authority and gives a partial, biased account of it. Wants to correct a historical lie she helped sustain without reducing her entire life to penance. Valuable combatant, not an exposition vending machine. An apparent late-rank prize who is politically inconvenient.
8. **Daran, the permanent successor to Dara and Tal (roughly 151–157).** One person, not an eighth simultaneously active body. Discussed below. Must acquire new relationships and tastes instead of becoming two old people delivered in one convenient package.

Any extra draw must earn substantial subsequent scenes. A new body is not the default reward for each boss. Existing companions can develop a profession, technique, disagreement, friendship, or leisure activity instead.

## 5. Power rules and economy: understandable, with room to grow

These are proposed rules to establish through observable consequences. Avoid early encyclopedia UI.

### Summoning and consent
- Ordinary summoners instantiate limited magical patterns: useful nonverbal constructs. Doyun's advanced work is still legitimate skill, not a morally inferior imitation of Taeo's power.
- Taeo has rare access to a living-transfer route. His first draw used an old emergency protocol that transported Nib without informed consent. This is a genuine wrong, not retconned into Nib secretly requesting it.
- Later investigation finds an invitation/response step before another living transfer. Randomness concerns which compatible adult candidate can answer through the route; a candidate can decline. No paid rerolls of an individual, duplicates of a person, forced contract, loyalty number, or obedience override.
- Taeo does not know this at the start. His decision not to draw again until he understands the risk is active caution with a material cost: another team earns money while he waits. Nib participates in the first invitation attempt.
- Why gacha persists: first contact explores unknown compatible routes; it cannot choose an individual. A declined invitation consumes that finite contact window, though no person is charged or punished. The next attempt requires another recovered alignment opportunity; it cannot be spammed. Later known-route travel and voluntary combat linking are separate operations, specified below. Recruitment retains uncertainty, anticipation and inconvenient outcomes.

### Funding without repetitive farming
- Two independent resources: ordinary money pays rent, wages, medicine, transport, insurance and equipment; stable anchor fragments recovered from boss territories support route expansion. Money cannot simply buy infinite drawing power.
- Anchor fragments are finite deposits shed into the terrain around a boss territory, recoverable during the approach while the boss is still alive. They are not corpse loot retrieved after a supposedly instantaneous closure. Certified teams already know how to extract and carry them. Show collection, withdrawal and the boss-clear closure in an unambiguous order. No permanent farming gate.
- A fragment must be integrated through a new stable route alignment, so repeat low-rank kills cannot produce unlimited roster expansion. Existing fragments can maintain equipment; recruitment progress requires qualitatively new access, not another thousand identical slimes.
- Integrations need observation and earned skill, not arbitrary 'bond points.' Favor discoveries from a rescue, a negotiated exchange, or a difficult new biome over daily quests.
- A small safe income develops by the late early arcs. Poverty is not renewed forever through random medical bills. Later constraints are scarce expertise, mutual obligations, response time, and legitimacy.

### Remote operations and fear
- Initially Taeo can maintain one cross-gate connection with intermittent sensory impressions only when the companion permits it. It is not effortless full-camera omniscience. Standard field radios work imperfectly and specialist relays become part of the team's skill.
- Agree a withdrawal signal and demonstrate a successful withdrawal before the first serious failure. Rescue cannot be a rule unveiled when convenient.
- Living companions can remain in the ordinary world without a humiliating daily 'upkeep' hostage mechanic. Once their arrival stabilizes, they are physically present people. Returning to a refuge/holding route is a later voluntary operation, not 'putting them away.'
- Cross-gate command reach and simultaneous high-intensity coordination have limits. Teach one limitation per operation. Growth comes from better relays, trusted autonomous leaders, and Taeo's increasingly powerful coordination, not his controlling more puppets per second.
- He rarely enters dungeons, and no climax must prove his courage by making him a sword hero. He can take meaningful physical risk on the outside during breaches, rescue civilians, and endure the terrifying uncertainty of waiting for a reply. Courage does not erase panic.
- By ~200, integrated command makes his freely cooperating force overwhelmingly stronger than any established rival. His own coordinated defensive field makes assassination implausible; do not keep throwing assassins at him as if nothing changed. Distance, incomplete information, consent, and competing civilian needs still matter.

### Injury and death
Living transferred people can be injured and die. No repeat draw restores the same person. Early injuries recover through time and treatment; do not demonstrate stakes by immediately killing a new recruit. The existing ordinary construct destruction/recreation rule is different and explicitly explained. A damaged communications link is not automatic death and cannot become the cliffhanger every arc. Establish survival information honestly rather than withholding a known rescue for several chapters.

### Fusion is an irreversible choice, not a loophole
- Fusion merges two compatible living anchors into one successor. Memories and dispositions blend into one point of view. Separate bodies and separate relationships cannot later be restored. No resurrection, costume change, alternating voices, or serial third-person stacking.
- Its tactical benefit is one new integrated ability; its costs include two independent positions and two distinct lives. The interface never calls it reversible. Consent is an ordinary serious conversation and demonstrated choice, not an extended legal seminar.
- Dara and Tal discover a temporary linking technique in 105–111: damage can cross their joined barrier, but healing cannot follow quickly enough to repair living wood. In 113–118 they learn permanent integration could support a new shared physiology. They initially decline because their separate lives are attractive.
- Their affirmative attraction develops before Tal is injured. Dara loves the inventiveness of field medicine and wants a body able to experience the living processes she now only treats. Tal enjoys cultivated spaces and physical sensation, and wants to create a moving habitat instead of always defending a fixed one. Both become curious about an actual successor who could discover things they cannot. This is not a promise that they will both get everything they want.
- Separate futures are pursued onscreen: Dara accepts a clinic teaching placement in 119–123 and enjoys being an excellent instructor; Tal begins a roof-garden cooperative in 121–127 and becomes its most opinionated resident. Neither is framed as a sad fallback. Sejin retires from raids successfully, with meaningful work, friendships and pleasure.
- Tal suffers permanent leg-root damage in Venn's ambush around 130 because Taeo commits to an insufficiently tested transport tactic; that decision and its repair persist regardless of fusion. Tal uses an adapted chair/platform and continues his garden leadership and shield work effectively. Dara is not conveniently given a parallel degenerative condition. Her reasons for fusion are exploratory and personal, not a cure.
- Around 151–157 they revisit the idea after independent time apart. Both could keep their existing satisfying lives. They choose the uncertain shared successor; some friends remain uncomfortable. The story acknowledges both discontinuity and continuity. Taeo does not order it or receive an ownership bonus. Their farewell gives separate relationships space; the successor's first scene is not a combat demonstration.

**Daran: an actual new person.** Proposed chosen name, selected by the successor. Carries Dara's quick blunt assessment and pleasure in teaching, and Tal's sensory attention and stubborn enjoyment of beautiful spaces. Emergent contradiction: eagerly welcomes strangers into a carefully tended environment, then cannot decide how much solitude to ask for. Neither predecessor was a compulsive host. Daran is curious, occasionally tactless, capable of grieving and glad to exist. They decline a prestigious combat-medical post both predecessors would have considered, and create a traveling civilian field-school to study unfamiliar habitats. Memories supply knowledge but not obligation to enact either old identity. One friend learns to ask what Daran wants rather than use Dara's nickname; another discovers a former favorite activity no longer fits. Later Daran initiates a friendship and makes a consequential decision unrelated to memorializing either predecessor. Nib's firm refusal ever to fuse is equally respected.

### First contact, travel and combat membership are different systems
| Operation | Input and limit | What it does / does not do |

Stronger/high-density anchors exceed early channel tolerance; Taeo cannot simply recruit an S-rank body at chapter 20. | Finds one unknown adult candidate capable of answering. Does not bind them or promise combat power. Stable arrival means a person can live independently. High-rank travelers still need wider physical endpoints; being present does not grant a high-bandwidth combat link. Taeo's practiced capacity and personal skill persist when someone leaves; missing a specialist still changes his tactical options. The roster is an ensemble and a trained team, not a metaphysical list of owned bodies.

### Boss-clear evacuation rule: choose this before the first raid
**Recommended forward rule, now specified rather than deferred:** boss death immediately closes inward entry and begins the dungeon's collapse. Every living entrant automatically received a one-use outward return mark when crossing. Boss death activates those marks for outward return to the original outside threshold. A conscious entrant can trigger theirs immediately; after 30 seconds the mark returns its bearer automatically, including unconscious or separated people. The marks operate through the dungeon boundary, independently of Taeo, field radios and the team's combat link. Already-dead bodies and worn/carried equipment return too; the mark does not heal or revive. Deliberately detaching a marked equipment item does not create another passenger mark. A native inhabitant who never crossed has no mark and needs an established transfer endpoint or evacuation through the still-open gate before the kill. Mass inside-native rescue therefore cannot be solved by killing the boss first.

Show this ordinary public rule in a controlled low-risk clear before any later climax relies on it. Test cases: separated Nib returns; unconscious supervised trainee returns; broken radio/combat link changes nothing; unmarked source resident does not return; an injury remains and needs treatment outside. Returning does not erase hazards or deaths incurred before the clear. One can also exit normally before boss death. Ordinary marks cannot be stored for another dungeon. No later villain may arbitrarily suppress them without advance evidence and a revised approved rule. The shop responder's chapter-1 explanation remains accurate; no opening changes are required.

### Concrete power ladder: why Taeo, and why only after practice
The faculty is **phase-linked force transfer**: Taeo can sense and, later, route a bounded portion of mechanical load through linked anchors. It is not telepathic omniscience, time travel, healing conversion, mind control or unlimited energy creation. His rare operator interface supplies this capability; technique, capacity and safe integration must be learned. Rapport improves communication, never a literal affection multiplier.

## 6. The 224-chapter causal spine

An arc's chapter window can move. Its emotional turn and resolution cannot be replaced by an announcement that stats increased. Quiet aftermath is part of each arc, not an apology before the next fight.

### I. Two people, one room — 1–9
**Mode:** intimate uncertainty with grounded comedy. **Immediate question:** what happens tomorrow, and can Nib go home? Chapters 1–2 remain untouched. Taeo visibly makes the assessment call; Nib overhears enough to insist on being present. They negotiate practical movement through the city through action: Nib steps outside, chooses to return when he sees how exposed he is, and objects when Taeo speaks over him. Seoryun enters their ordinary media environment. The assessment confirms that Nib's responses are independent, while Taeo's raw output is unimpressive. A controlled exercise demonstrates one useful coordination sensation, not a full power catalogue. **Choice:** Taeo proposes a modest supervised trial that could pay them while testing whether remote work is viable; Nib makes participation conditional on visiting a recorded landscape that may help his own search. Taeo rejects an exclusive observation offer because it would foreclose the independent method he wants to build. **Resolution:** they have a short-term plan, an appointment completed, and a disagreement still alive. **Next pull:** Nib wants to see a dungeon because one recorded landscape resembles a place he knows. Do not guarantee it is his home.

### II. The first route — 10–20
**Mode:** cautious expedition and a small professional rivalry. **Immediate question:** can Nib cross and return safely while Taeo remains outside? Minho's team provides legitimate supervision. First rehearsal outside and a short successful crossing establish the withdrawal rule. Nib initially performs worse than a standard construct at simple heavy work; his independent choice becomes valuable when he notices airflow through a blocked passage. **Choice:** when ordered to finish a cheap collection objective, Taeo backs Nib's decision to investigate signs of a separated worker, paying a contract penalty. The rescue is small, messy, and achieved with the licensed team, not a solo boss slaughter. **Resolution:** worker recovered; licensed hunters kill the boss and the gate closes; Nib's modest pay buys something he chooses himself. Taeo apologizes for a bad instruction without a philosophy speech. **Consequence:** Taeo states a new goal he owns: clear a paying route without entering it, then buy a base no client can evict them from. He chooses a target and drafts a practical trial. Evidence enables the first invitation attempt; income is not suddenly solved.

### III. The route he chooses — 21–33
**Mode:** an inconvenient draw, invention and a clean tactical win. Taeo wants a light climber for a narrow deposit route; the unpredictable invitation brings Orrin, a heavy stoneback who cannot fit. Orrin's preference for a real home matters, but the arc is not a maintenance problem. Taeo refuses to write off his own idea: he chooses a different terraced route, studies safe weight-transfer tests, and plans a two-anchor strike. Minho wins the central route honestly; Taeo never entered that contest. **Immediate question:** can Taeo's method win the peripheral deposit he deliberately selected? He spends saved money on test materials, makes one small bad assumption and corrects it before the raid. Nib and Orrin execute his prepared bridge-boss maneuver, redirecting a small impact fraction at the right moment. **Choice:** Taeo takes a calculated paid risk his family would prefer he avoid. **Resolution:** the team wins its actual objective without discovering a victim, gets its resource, and exits under the previously demonstrated return-mark rule. Their first satisfying surplus secures a workshop yard with separate rooms. **Consequence:** Taeo's remote-clear method has proof, a financial reward and a next technical problem. Minho respects the result without becoming a follower. No S-rank secretly carried this victory.

### IV. The captain who comes back — 34–47
**Mode:** disaster rescue and an idol encounter. Seoryun is already leading the difficult part of a multi-entry incident. Taeo's crew is assigned a modest flank. **Immediate question:** can an unglamorous side passage keep the evacuation moving? His map becomes wrong after structural change; he admits it, and Nib improvises with field training. Seoryun saves Taeo's exposed outside position, making the rank gap visceral. **Choice:** he accepts her order to abandon valuable salvage rather than trying to prove he belongs. **Resolution:** their sector evacuates; the operation succeeds because several teams do different jobs. Seoryun remembers Nib's name as well as Taeo's. In a brief aftermath conversation Taeo admits why the old broadcast mattered. She tells him about one real failure, without becoming cynical. **Consequence:** he gains an introduction to a training exchange, not an S-rank sponsor who solves his life. Sera's invitation begins toward the arc's end, with her search goal complicating the next job.

### V. A place in the photograph — 48–62
**Mode:** public competition, social exposure and two distinct results. A guild exhibition offers equipment grants and a chance to place a remote summoner's name on a real professional record. Taeo enters because he wants both. Doyun beats his rigid method in a standardized timed event by changing direction faster than he can reassign force; no convenient accident reverses their moral standing. Taeo is disappointed, studies the loss, and asks for a technical rematch later. Sunline's pursuit of her own aerial record gives her more important priorities than mentoring him. **Choice:** Taeo enters a separate adaptive obstacle event with a reservoir device he developed through safe testing, even though withdrawing would protect his emerging reputation. **Resolution:** the bank-and-release maneuver wins that event outright; he accepts a real award, then shares the photo and credit because the team earned it. Nib dislikes publicity; Sera enjoys it. That disagreement does not cancel the pleasure of winning. **Consequence:** the device's visible saturation after cushioning a fall establishes a defensive precursor and a new limit. Taeo now wants to extend the method beyond a single gate, while public attention makes his next failure harder to admit.

### VI. The river without a sky — 63–80
**Mode:** beautiful, unsettling exploration; a real combat set piece. Kesh joins, and the crew enters an underground river route to identify an old settlement sign associated with Sera's search. **Immediate question:** why does a repeated warning appear to come from an upstream team that denies sending it? They compare clocks first, ruling out a timestamp offset. Taeo has Kesh send a harmless numbered test pulse while Nib physically opens and closes one relay path; repetition stops when that path is broken. The narrower answer is a delayed echo traveling around a loop, not time reversal. It changes their route choice before the predator uses the same echo path to distract them. A predatory boss uses false responses to split teams. Taeo nearly gets Sera killed by trusting the interface over her observation. **Choice:** Taeo abandons his first route, then authors a staggered relay and firing plan from their measured echo and force-transfer tests. Kesh and Nib make the field arrangement work; Taeo commits the stored wave at the interval he calculated and Sera makes the shot. He is responsible for a new successful tactic, not only for getting out of his companions’ way. **Resolution:** boss clear and closure; they recover a dated message that narrows the brother's whereabouts. **Consequence:** the old communications infrastructure is undeniable. The team wants a guild of its own so their methods and recovery time cannot be overruled by clients. Founding follows earned demand, not arbitrary minimum-level unlock.

### VII. Their name over the door — 81–98
**Mode:** founding, competitive acquisition and commercial rivalry. Taeo's goal is a permanent solvent guild whose members choose jobs, with his remote-clear method as a market advantage. Dara joins and critiques their field practice. Baek offers a merger that genuinely solves financing but assigns control of their technical work to his board. Taeo does not reject it on principle and hope money appears: he identifies a public competitive contract for an anchor field, calculates the sale of its finite recoverable stock and a follow-on nonexclusive logistics contract and enters with an independent financing partner. Minho chooses a different deal for his family guild. **Immediate question:** can Taeo win the resource that makes independence affordable? His team studies a stronger rival's displacement tactic; he develops a two-threshold force exchange and tests its recoil limit. **Choice:** he commits most of their available capital, over Nib's preference for a smaller secure job. The risk is ambitious, not automatically virtuous. **Climax:** they win the actual field contest by redirecting pressure into a prepared counterstroke. No rescue interrupts the competition and no judge gifts them the prize for kindness. **Resolution:** the finite resource sale pays for the base and the logistics contract supports the clinic arrangement; Taeo's method is visibly superior at this problem. **Consequence:** their access attracts Venn's interference. A diverted shipment harms a real buyer and exposes a measurable anchor defect. A background district crisis is resolved by Baek's guild without Taeo, preserving the wider world's competence.

### VIII. People on the other bank — 99–118
**Mode:** border expedition and reunion with conflicting wishes. Tal arrives from the source network; Sera's brother is found alive in an inhabited refuge, already committed to people there. **Immediate question:** does 'bring him home' mean what Sera assumed? The refuge can be contacted through a dungeon-adjacent transfer node; clearing the dungeon still closes its combat gate. Nib finally receives verifiable news of his own community, including changed leadership and a friend whose new settlement cannot yet be reached. **Choice:** Taeo funds an expedition to map a luminous canopy route described by Tal rather than taking the safer commercial job his investors prefer. The team wants the discovery for its own sake. Orrin and Tal help recover a living landscape record that reveals a refuge contact; it costs a damaged reservoir and a delayed profitable job. A later known-route courier uses that contact, distinct from a first-contact draw. The climax is the beautiful discovery and Sera’s difficult reunion, not a rescue that earns another referral. Sera respects her brother's decision to remain, after a genuine argument. **Resolution:** the sibling search ends with contact and a future relationship, not another kidnapping. **Consequence:** there are living source societies, and recruiting draws have been crossing an old emergency network. Tal and Dara encounter fusion records and initially reject the option. The refuge council shows a dated Ilyra closure order, accepts one earlier closure as necessary, and disputes the next; this is a live political problem before she appears personally. Mara and Ibrahim now have practical reasons to challenge purely private control of the route.

### IX. A victory that belongs to someone else — 119–136
**Mode:** coalition rivalry, an ambitious mistake and a lasting tactical change. Viktor's force wins a spectacular international battle through a prepared doctrine Taeo cannot yet match. Taeo contributes but receives no final-command consolation. He wants to prove his newer cross-threshold method can outdo a fixed military formation. Meanwhile Dara begins a teaching placement she enjoys; Tal establishes a roof-garden cooperative and likes its civilian life. Those are actual attractive futures before fusion.

Venn seeks monopoly over unstable transport endpoints and uses modified anchors to evade inspections. His market position was established in 88–98; he is not simply collecting rare powers. He lures Taeo toward a mobile archive relay by apparently exposing a shipment. **Choice:** Taeo accelerates a relay switch that passed only static tests, hoping to capture Venn and demonstrate a prestigious new maneuver before Viktor does. Venn's counter-pulse exploits the known transition gap. Tal suffers permanent leg-root injury; the shipment escapes and public footage contradicts Taeo's optimistic risk claims. This loss is irreversible and causally his, not an enemy teleporting in with a new rule.

**Resolution:** the crew survives through established return and field methods, but the failure remains a failure. Taeo releases the test record, pays compensation from the base reserve and spends time repairing his method. Tal continues gardening and barrier work with an adapted platform; Dara refuses to let their earlier fusion discussion be treated as a convenient cure. Seoryun supports suspending this unproven transfer pattern, not confiscating everyone for their own good. **Consequence:** branch isolation becomes a necessary engineering goal, and Taeo has a specific rival technique to defeat. Venn's active pursuit occupies this arc and the next, not forty chapters of inconsequential escapes. Meret's invitation supplies partial evidence of source authorities whose closure orders have already affected known places.

### X. The two who will not remain two — 137–157
**Mode:** factional pursuit, intimate fracture, then a deliberate irreversible choice. Meret reveals that the source authority is divided; she participated in a border closure and is distrusted by refugees. An independent refugee account confirms that one Ilyra closure preserved an inhabited shelf, even while contesting its abandoned perimeter. Nib disagrees with Taeo over a mission connected to his home and sits it out; the team must operate without his particular skill. This causes an actual missed opportunity, not a punishment engineered to prove Nib should obey. **Immediate external problem:** Venn tries to seize a recoverable relay archive before a legitimate closure. Taeo deliberately offers the same apparent relay opening, but now routes the counter-pulse into an isolated reservoir. Minho and the companions execute the moving capture; Venn is taken with the vehicle and archive intact, not replaced by an identical larger broker. This is Taeo’s concrete tactical answer to his earlier mistake. **Choice:** Taeo chooses to pursue Venn despite Nib declining that mission, and builds a workable substitute plan rather than manipulating him into agreement. Nib undertakes his own reconnaissance visit with a separately guaranteed return arrangement. Dara and Tal's fusion decision occurs after the mission, not under a battle countdown. **Resolution:** Venn's commercial threat ends, the archive is saved, Nib makes his round trip and finds that friend alive with a new family at the now-reachable settlement, and Daran begins a new life. **Consequence:** archive evidence identifies the network's real emergency and its human-scale history. The final conflict is no longer an unknown cosmic menace.

### XI. The country behind the doors — 158–176
**Mode:** diplomatic adventure, falsifiable tests and a border battle. Earlier evidence has made **Ilyra Sen**, the source security commander, an identifiable policymaker before her personal entrance: a dated closure order recovered near 103, a refuge council disputing its schedule near 112, and independent evidence near 140 that one of her earlier closures really saved a populated shelf. She leads no universal evil empire. The source network was built by endangered worlds to preserve populations through spatial collapse. Dungeons are unstable pressure outlets and abandoned operational zones; boss organisms concentrate local anchors. Killing a boss closes that local connection, not an entire country.

**Immediate problem:** Ilyra must protect a basin of roughly 30,000 people. Her prepared isolation plan can secure most of it but abandons an outer district. Taeo's first alternative demonstration handles only a small town; it fails in the basin's crystalline ground because feedback multiplies. She is correct that a successful small trial proves neither geology tolerance nor mass capacity. She provides a test site and a deadline rather than refusing evidence out of pride. Taeo initially fails it onscreen.

**Choice:** Taeo commits to solving the specific feedback problem, risking commercial resources and his reputation rather than demanding trust. Seoryun helps define a test that would convince her too. Paired independent relays cancel measured background noise; his team then proves the system under a full local evacuation load. **Resolution:** one town moves safely and the central mystery receives firm answers: rare compatible operator activation; emergency transport that wronged Nib; ordinary constructs as separate technology; people with conflicting policies behind the interface. No bloodline or hidden god appears. **Consequence:** Ilyra accepts the local result but identifies two still-unsolved conditions: full-basin peak capacity and survival after a relay fails during transfer. Taeo must meet those conditions before the isolation deadline. Her objection is now a testable campaign goal.

### XII. An empire is made of places — 177–194
**Mode:** a three-part campaign with different desired outcomes. The guild has become an attractive federation of workshops, clinics, safe districts and independent crews; its scale appears in places and capabilities. Taeo sets a public goal: demonstrate a full alternative before Ilyra's scheduled isolation. Each site solves a measured constraint, not an arbitrary key hunt.

1. **Flooded transit yard:** provides reservoir volume for the basin's measured peak load. Mara wants access to its unexplored deeper route in return for her expertise. The team defeats the yard guardian and wins the needed equipment; the payoff is resource and discovery, not another reputation rescue.
2. **Populated industrial shelf:** its operators already run stabilizers. They will not accept a plan that puts their homes at risk while others escape. Taeo and Ibrahim negotiate a bounded load-sharing trial with backup supplies, and the operators win enforceable local control. The dramatic climax is a successful stress test and a hard-won agreement; no secretly corrupt foreman needs to be killed.
3. **Exposed aerial relay:** introduces redundancy that keeps transfers safe after one site fails. Jiwon gets a legitimate attempt at her aerial clear record, while Taeo must choose whether to interrupt her attempt when readings change. He waits through a previously tested safe interval, trusting evidence rather than bravado. She succeeds, and the final trial visibly survives a deliberate branch outage.

**Choice:** Taeo commits these resources to a real public demonstration instead of hoarding them as a private weapon. That choice also builds his combat lattice. Nib has returned by choice and leads a pivotal ground crew; Minho and Seoryun hold independent command. **Resolution:** the coalition proves its measured capacity and fault tolerance, and Taeo exceeds an established S-rank peak-force benchmark. Ilyra's remaining concern is no longer the disproven local constraints: basin weather is now changing faster than the static trial, and the emergency isolation mechanism will lock irreversibly within hours. Her constituency will not accept a live gamble on all 30,000 people. She proposes securing the inner basin immediately, knowingly sacrificing the outer district; Taeo offers a staged live transfer that could save it too. **Consequence:** she orders her strongest established force to enforce the lock. Her fight defends a culpable choice under a real uncertainty, not an inability to count successful tests. The coalition must win space and time without destroying the machinery both populations need.

### XIII. My summons go first — 195–207
**Mode:** earned power spectacle followed by a consequential decision. Taeo's distributed coordination reaches its full form around 198–201. The audience sees the prior skills combine: Orrin's stable platforms, Kesh's relays, Sera's precision, Daran's integrated healing defense, Meret's route knowledge, Nib's command and the human coalition's independent action. No sudden tenth summon supplies the winning ability. Ilyra’s elite force attempts dispersion, concentrated impact and its known counter-pulse; Taeo defeats each through previously demonstrated lattice operations and an intact measured reserve. His disabling counterstrike ends their military resistance decisively. Taeo remains outside the core dungeon directing and shielding the inhabited threshold. He is afraid and visibly effective.

**Immediate battle:** Ilyra commits her established strongest force to seal the coalition out. Taeo defeats that force decisively; do not drag superiority out with four surprise forms. The remaining problem is that taking sole master control would stabilize the network quickest while making every source settlement dependent on him. **Choice:** after winning the battle, he must choose between personal central control that can secure the inner basin immediately and a distributed live transfer that needs six more hours while roughly 3,000 outer-district residents remain in reinforced shelters. Sole control is the faster lower-variance option for the inner basin but makes later transit dependent on him and does not retrieve the abandoned district. Distribution can include them, with a small real failure risk under changing weather. Local leaders, including Ilyra’s opposing council, hear the measured risk and choose to participate; Taeo cannot authorize it for everyone. Allies supply the shelter defense and reserve power during those six hours. One reservoir and an inhabited district’s buildings are permanently lost, while its evacuated people survive; the cost is concrete displacement and rebuilding, not a casualty inserted to punish the ethical option. Taeo uses his unique adaptive control to complete the transition, then leaves independent anchors operating. This is costly in exclusive authority, time, resources and risk, not a sneaky total power reset. He remains unmatched through his established voluntary coalition and skill. **Resolution:** forced emergency conscription ends, instability is brought under sustainable control, Ilyra survives to answer for her decisions and help her population, and Nib can choose long-term residence without a wartime emergency obligation. The chapter title's meaning has shifted through behavior: companions go first because they possess the relevant courage and skills, and eventually because Taeo has made sure there is a way back.

### XIV. The way home — 208–224
**Mode:** three substantial aftermath units, not a career-checklist tour: (208–213) the displaced district’s settlement dispute and rebuilding choice; (214–220) Nib’s chosen residence and Daran’s field-school decision; (221–224) the junior team’s modest adventure and Taeo’s family commitment. Do not substitute a new cosmic enemy for resolution. Some companions choose long visits or permanent departure; some remain. Daran chooses a name and civic role on their own timetable. Nib returns to a changed community, visits the friend whose whereabouts were resolved on his earlier round trip, and discovers that his future need not be a restoration of his old place. He later returns to Taeo's city for his own professional and personal reasons, not because the outside world rejected him. Seoryun can finally hand over a rescue shift without feeling she abandoned it. Minho's independent guild thrives without becoming Taeo's subordinate. Money is no longer the family's main problem, and Taeo keeps a promised visit.

A last small gate operation lets the reader recognize how far the team has come: juniors handle much of it, the old companions have distinctive roles, Taeo leaves before the debrief to attend the family occasion because a competent deputy exists. Close with ordinary life enlarged by earned possibilities. The final image can echo the first small room through a busy table and an open place for a returning friend, but must not claim that the Chapter 2 cup contained a hidden code. No resurrection of all losses, time reset, or reclassification of the whole story as a training simulation.
