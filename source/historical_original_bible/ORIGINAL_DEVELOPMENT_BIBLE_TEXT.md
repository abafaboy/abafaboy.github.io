Modern Dungeon Summoner Story
Bible
An original modern fantasy manhwa
Development edition • October 2026
A cautious young worker summons a creature too weak to impress anyone. While
other hunters enter dungeons, he learns how to bring a growing family of
summons home alive. Their victories become a guild, their sanctuary becomes a
city, and their alliances eventually become an empire.
This bible provides the story engine, proposed laws of magic and ecology,
character relationships, a roughly 200-chapter progression, and practical
continuity rules for writing the series. Its core recommendation is to make
strength emerge from knowledge, trust and difficult decisions before allowing the
protagonist to become unrivaled.
The emotional promise is simple: the person who starts with almost nothing
learns to build a home powerful enough to protect others. The adventure promise
is equally important: strange dungeons, dangerous monsters, unpredictable
summons, clever victories and spectacular earned upgrades.
All names, places, numbers and specific plot mechanics below are
proposals unless explicitly identified as confirmed. The document is a
development foundation, not a declaration that every choice is settled.

The confirmed brief and open choices
Confirmed foundation
The protagonist is an ordinary, cautious young worker. He is an orphan, lives
alone in a tiny apartment, and has a sibling living elsewhere with their
grandparents. He remains cautious about dungeons for a long time and relies on
summons rather than rapidly becoming a fearless front-line hunter. A first
personal entry is not yet fixed.
His summoning uses a gacha element. He begins with a very weak mythical
creature, with goblin and slime still under consideration, and eventually
commands multiple creatures and heroes. Relationships with these beings
matter. His rise should be slow and earned, reaching unrivaled strength around
the 200-chapter scale. His organization grows from a guild into an empire while
the world remains modern.
Fusion is permanent. It creates a new person whose personality blends the
originals. It is not a costume change, a temporary team attack or a reversible
combination.

Choices still open
The protagonist's name, age, occupation, country and exact family history
remain open. So do the series title, first summon, final cast, romance, power
terminology and numerical tuning. This document uses role labels to avoid
prematurely locking character names.
The chosen visual direction combines the expressive faces and comic timing of
The Greatest Estate Developer with the clean proportions and dramatic action
lighting of Omniscient Reader. These are broad visual references, not permission
to copy existing character designs. Original character sheets and images remain
to be developed.

How to use the proposals
Read the first eleven sections before scripting combat. Read the cast and
opening episodes before scripting dialogue. Use the arc plan as a sequence of
earned changes, with chapter ranges adjustable during serialization. Preserve the
causal order even if episodes move.

The core story engine
A home at the edge of the impossible
A low-paid worker comes home exhausted to a room too small for the life he
wants. An anomalous summoning invitation gives him neither a heroic physique
nor a legendary champion. It gives him one frightened, inconvenient creature
and a responsibility he cannot comfortably afford.
He initially wants practical things: enough money to stop falling behind, a safer
future for his sibling, and a companion who will return when sent into danger.
The ambition to build an empire grows out of repeatedly discovering that
personal safety is fragile when the surrounding world is unsafe.

The repeating adventure loop
A dungeon presents a visible objective and a hidden ecological problem. The
protagonist studies what he can, assembles a small team and makes a plan. A
gacha result, rival or unexpected monster complicates it. The summons
improvise as people, not obedient equipment. Success produces resources and
knowledge; injury, grief or public consequences prevent a clean reset. The return
home changes a relationship and opens the next problem.
Every arc needs at least one satisfying dungeon conquest, one useful or
emotionally difficult summon development, and one decision the protagonist
cannot outsource. Administration belongs between these moments, rarely in
their place.

The central tension
He can become the strongest commander without becoming the bravest person
in a room. His weakness is not caution itself. It is the temptation to treat distance
as innocence: if he never enters, can he pretend he is not choosing who takes
the risk? His growth is toward shared responsibility, honest consent and better
preparation, without erasing his fear or turning him into a sword-wielding
substitute for the cast.

The laws of the world
Proposed canon. The following laws constrain protagonists, enemies and
institutions equally. Reveal them through visible consequences before characters
explain them.
   Matter has an origin. Bodies, food, equipment and loot require physical
    material or transport from an existing place. Mana cannot supply missing
    atoms.
   Useful energy is spent. Casting, transfer and conversion have losses.
    Discharged material does not recharge by crossing a gate.
   Information is separate from cargo. A bond can send a warning without
    carrying a cart of ore. Seeing a destination does not unlock teleportation.
   Dungeons persist. Parties visit the same habitats. Damage, migration,
    settlements and past mistakes remain after a chapter ends.
   Time normally agrees. An hour inside is an hour outside. No routine
    training chamber grants years of free growth.
   Attention is finite. More allies require delegation, not infinitely faster
    thought. Autonomous competence is a major upgrade.
   Life needs care. Summons need suitable food, rest, treatment and safe
    accommodation. Dismissal never resets injuries.
   Death remains death. A later summon of the same species is a different
    individual. A surviving memory is not resurrection.
   Fusion changes personhood. Two separate people become one
    irreversible integrated new person with blended traits and the right to
    change. The story need not declare this equivalent to ordinary death.
   Danger is multidimensional. Combat power does not prevent drowning,
    starvation, poor judgment or dispersed civilian casualties.
   Society notices evidence. Neighbors notice noise; authorities notice
    repeatable anomalies; markets react to large supplies.
   Victory does not run a city. The strongest fighter still needs engineers,
    caregivers, food suppliers and legitimate agreements.
A newly introduced exception must cost something, be foreshadowed and remain
available to opponents under the same conditions. Never invent a rule only to
rescue the protagonist from a deserved consequence.

What dungeons really are
The hidden author level explanation
Dungeons are compartments in a damaged interworld habitat and pressure-
management network. Some contain constructed chambers; others preserve
pieces of landscapes. The builders are absent or fragmented. Gates connect
Earth to existing places, rather than generating a fresh level for each visitor.
The proposed opening is eighteen years after the first gates in a fictional
contemporary coastal metropolis. This allows a recognizable industry to exist
without pretending its science is complete. A fictional republic avoids passing
invented legislation off as the laws of a real country. The city, calendar and
country remain open choices.
The summoning interface is a restricted legacy access system. Its draw format
reflects uncertain contact and compatibility, not a benevolent author secretly
choosing the perfect teammate. The protagonist receives access through
contingent compatibility and timing. He does not need a royal bloodline or
parents murdered by the final villain.

Gate lifecycle
A seep produces weak anomalies. Formation creates a measurable throat.
Surveyors test air, geometry, contamination and pressure. An open phase
supports travel and extraction. Stress increases when vents fail, a migration
crowds the entrance or reckless extraction damages the regulator. Stabilization
requires repair or managed shutdown. A break releases organisms, debris and
pressure into the surrounding city.
Forecasts are ranges with uncertainty. There is no universal countdown. A
second pressure reading can save more lives than a dramatic promise.

Bosses are not universal switches
A boss may be an apex animal, a guardian or a sentient regulator operator.
Killing it can grant access to an anchor; it does not magically certify every room
as safe. Some bosses maintain stability. An early apparently triumphant kill
should worsen a gate, establishing the difference between clearing an obstacle
and understanding a place.

Ecology that creates adventures
Food webs with visible consequences
Dark habitats begin with chemical gradients, water, nutrients and microbial films.
Real chemosynthetic ecosystems provide the research analogy; mana-assisted
root mats are fictional. Small grazers eat the films. Scavengers and smaller
predators eat the grazers. Sparse apex animals depend on the lower layers.
Decomposers return nutrients while energy is lost as heat.
This is a story tool, not a biology lecture. A tunnel full of carnivores should have
prey, imported food, dormancy or a recent migration. Empty feed pits can
foreshadow a starving boss. A missing microbial mat can predict a displaced
swarm before the first attack.

Three recurring habitats
Mossworks: warm water-management chambers, metal-smelling films, insects,
small slimes and salvage goblins. Blind corners make its low combat rank
misleading. It teaches retreat routes, cheap equipment and the value of noticing
small changes.
Root Cathedral: an old agricultural habitat with great root pillars, grazers,
pollinators and communities controlling fresh water. Fire wins a fight but can
destroy the food base. A rescue and a harvest can become incompatible goals.
Glass Estuary: abrasive tidal flats, salt-adapted organisms, charged mineral
growth and migratory armored fauna. Tides, corrosion and damaged transport
challenge even powerful teams. This becomes a natural stage for the guild's first
infrastructure-scale victory.

No convenient respawn
Return visits reveal migration, reproduction, regrowth or machinery rebuilding
guardians from finite feedstock. Dead individuals do not respawn. Overharvesting
can collapse a habitat and send hungry animals toward the gate.
Dungeon meat requires testing. Quarantine protects both worlds. A slime cannot
harmlessly digest every industrial poison; a goblin cannot live on snacks
indefinitely. These constraints create useful civilian characters and brief domestic
comedy without replacing the hunt.

What summoning actually does
A person arrives
A summon is a living migrant transferred through the old network, not a
disposable copy manufactured from a legend. Some look like small monsters;
others are humanoids or former heroes. Mythical is Earth's classification. Their
bodies, pasts and preferences exist before the protagonist meets them.
The proposed first arrival is a fixed emergency transfer from a one-use reserve. It
is deliberately weak and does not count toward random-draw guarantees. This
preserves the chosen beginning without pretending that an improbable
legendary opening was impossible. Goblin or slime remains undecided.
Later draws contact a willing, compatible migration population. A selected
individual can refuse. Nobody becomes property, and a same-species arrival is
never a spare copy of a lost friend. Conventional travelers can also join the
community, although joining does not grant a free magical covenant.

Four working ledgers
Money pays for rent, meals, staff, equipment and transport. It cannot purchase
draws or obedience.
Bound fragments record an attributed share of a genuinely stabilized dungeon
gradient. They fund recruitment, covenant repair and relay construction. They
cannot be transferred between accounts or manufactured from ordinary
electricity.
Combat charge powers abilities, links and recall. It comes from finite stores and
recovery, with rate limits and losses.
Adaptation records what a person has actually learned and integrated. It cannot
be donated, stolen by killing someone, or converted into money.
Physical supplies sit beneath all four ledgers: bodies still need food, equipment
still needs material and habitats still have finite stocks. The interface does not
replace warehouses.

The scarcity that matters
A proposed draw costs 100 fragments. A modest successful outing might yield 20
to this team's attributed share, with 10 reserved for repair and infrastructure.
Ten such outings fund a draw. The event's finite pool is divided among
contributors; observers cannot multiply it by attending. Draws are exciting
precisely because they are occasional choices.

Gacha odds and honest guarantees
All probabilities are proposed tuning values. Rarity describes dormant
magical architecture, not moral worth or immediate fighting strength. Arrival
level 1 measures adaptation to the new environment, not the absence of useful
power. A rare arrival can immediately demonstrate a striking signature ability,
with stamina, precision and advanced applications still to develop.
   Hearth: 75%. A narrow, inexpensive magical architecture.
   Thread: 20%. Related capabilities or a demanding specialty.
   Crest: 4.5%. Complex potential for later team-scale effects.
   Horizon: 0.5%. Exceptional architecture with demanding maturation needs.
A common goblin can become a remarkable engineer. A rare arrival offers a
genuine unusual advantage but still needs training, resources and a place to
sleep. Do not advertise a thrilling rare result and then make it entirely useless for
fifty chapters.

Two persistent counters
The first counter tracks draws since the last Crest or Horizon. After nineteen
without either, the twentieth guarantees one: 90% Crest and 10% Horizon. Either
result resets that counter.
The second tracks draws since the last Horizon. After ninety-nine without one,
the hundredth guarantees Horizon and resets both counters. This guarantee
takes priority over the other. A Crest resets only the first counter; Hearth and
Thread advance both.
There are no expiring banners, paid rerolls, secret rate boosts or limited-time
pressure. The price, relevant odds and both counters appear before the decision.
The fixed starter is excluded.

Consent without a reroll loophole
Candidates opt into migration cohorts. If a rarity class has no eligible candidates,
drawing is unavailable before payment; advertised odds do not silently change.
An interrupted transfer preserves its selection. A withdrawing candidate is
protected, but refusal grants neither bonus pity progress nor a free reroll.
Completion waits for a valid transfer or the price is refunded.
Healing, tools and ordinary recruitment remain alternatives to rolling. Saving a
friend's treatment budget should feel admirable, not like failing to understand
the genre.

Probability and resource sanity checks
What ten draws really mean
On ordinary draws, the combined chance of Crest or Horizon is 5%. From fresh
counters, the chance of at least one by draw n, for n below twenty, is 1 minus
0.95 raised to n.
   Five draws: 22.62% chance of at least one Crest or Horizon.
   Ten draws: 40.13%.
   Nineteen draws: 62.26%.
   Twenty draws: 100%, because the guarantee applies if needed.
The average waiting time from fresh counters is 12.8303 draws: the sum of 0.95
raised to powers zero through nineteen. At 100 fragments per draw, the
expected cost is approximately 1,283 fragments and the maximum is 2,000 for
this combined rarity event. An average never promises a particular character a
result on schedule.

Horizon has different mathematics
Before a forced draw, its ordinary chance is 0.5%: approximately 2.48% to
appear within five draws and 4.89% within ten. Do not extend the constant-rate
formula beyond the first pity intervention. The guaranteed Crest-or-Horizon draw
has a 10% Horizon chance, and the hundredth-draw guarantee changes the
process again.
For an audit, track probability across both counters. On each draw, apply the
hundredth rule first, the twentieth rule second, and otherwise the ordinary odds.
Route Horizon to zero on both counters, Crest to zero on the first only, and lower
results forward on both.

Story budgeting
A proposed cumulative recruitment budget is one random arrival or fewer by
chapter 20, three or four by 40, six to eight by 70, twelve to fifteen by 100,
twenty-five to thirty by 140, and sixty to seventy by 200. Most late arrivals are
community members or supporting specialists, not sixty protagonists competing
for dialogue.
The hundredth guarantee need not be reached in this saga. Do not speed-run
recruitment simply to display every mechanic.

Remote command without remote invulnerability
Early range and attention
The starter link supports one stable dungeon boundary and at most 300 metres
of effective route length. Proposed one-way delay is two seconds. The
protagonist works from a protected exterior staging location; he cannot supervise
his first expedition from his apartment across town.
He receives one detailed sensory feed at a time, with that companion's
agreement. Other active links carry brief intentional messages and coarse
condition reports. There is no mind reading, lie detector, automatic map or view
through unexplored rooms. Full attention to a feed makes driving, crossing
streets or doing a paid work shift unsafe.
An improved personal route can reach three kilometres after training and relay
installation. Maintained regional hops extend it further. Each relay adds at least
half a second one-way, plus measurable interference. Longer reach creates
travel, maintenance and defense problems worth solving.

Links are support capacity
An ordinary body's proposed load is 1 plus the integer part of its level minus 1
divided by 20. Early personal capacity is 3; later planning milestones are 8, 18
and finally 36. A fused body retains at least the predecessors' combined
structural load.
People can physically travel without active support. Capacity limits
communication, recall access and routed charge, not how many citizens are
allowed to exist. Ordinary transport can move a larger force, honestly trading
away magical support rather than exploiting a forbidden loophole.

Field leadership matters
Every mission has a field leader, retreat threshold, rendezvous and contact-loss
plan. Summons may disagree and act independently. If the protagonist falls
asleep or loses consciousness, his personal feeds stop; real bodies remain, and
charged infrastructure lasts only for its stated reserve.
Late allied operators run local networks. They expand strategic reach without
giving one human every soldier's senses. Trust is both an emotional achievement
and a technical necessity.

Retreat injury and death
Recall is an extraction procedure
A surviving covenant route allows recall to a registered refuge after ten
uninterrupted seconds of transfer formation. Proposed cost is two combat-charge
units per load unit, followed by a thirty-minute route cooldown. Being struck
during formation interrupts it. An ally may shield the person, making teamwork
essential.
Only the person and declared worn equipment within the registered load travel.
Ore, large freight and unregistered passengers leave through an ordinary gate or
an established freight anchor. No inventory-sized army, explosive delivery
loophole or truck hidden under a coat.
The person must survive until formation finishes. Transfer itself is momentary
and cannot become permanent invulnerability. Charge, cooldown and route
availability must be known before a scene depends on them.

Healing preserves consequences
Recall does not reset wounds, fatigue, disease or spent abilities. Fusion also
preserves injury and depleted reserves. Treatment takes appropriate resources
and recovery. A disabled or recovering companion remains a person with
relationships and useful choices, not a temporarily useless item.
The interface distinguishes route lost, vital status unknown and confirmed death.
A reunion can follow honest uncertainty. It cannot undo an authoritative death
notice solely because the character became popular.

Real residence
The apartment houses only what physically fits. A later refuge occupies real
space with water, food, waste and neighbors. Time continues there. No one waits
indefinitely in a timeless inventory.
If the protagonist dies, his companions do not disappear. Personal routes fail and
local reserves eventually run out, but the community survives or fails through the
institutions it built. Preparing for that possibility is a mature expression of care,
and a late reason to train successors rather than hoard every decision.

Growth that keeps the first victories relevant
Practice before spectacle
Proposed levels run from 1 to 100, but the reader should see meaningful
thresholds rather than constant arithmetic. A transition from level L to the next
costs 5 plus twice L adaptation credits. The total from level 1 to N is (N minus 1)
times (N plus 5): 135 credits to level 10, 475 to level 20 and 3,835 to level 60.
Credits represent integrated practice. Recovery consolidates gains. Repeating an
easy task improves efficiency before it plateaus; killing harmless creatures does
not manufacture mastery. The commander develops observation, judgment and
communication. A companion's sword practice does not magically strengthen his
arms.
At levels 20, 40, 60 and 80, advancement also requires a compatible structural
resource. Discovery, trade, rescue or engineering can obtain it. Every threshold
need not become a mandatory boss organ.

An author side comparison tool
For sustained magical output only, use base output multiplied by [1 plus 0.06
times (level minus 1)] and by 1.8 for each completed structural transition. With
base output 1, level 10 is 1.54; level 30 after one transition is about 4.93; level
60 after two is about 14.71.
This is a balancing aid, not a damage law. Armor, speed, accuracy, range and
fatigue remain separate. Do not multiply every bodily trait by the same number
or declare bullet immunity solely from a level label.

What unrivaled means
The endpoint is unrivaled summoning and battle power in the known world. The
protagonist together with his developed roster decisively defeats the strongest
rival force, including an assembled coalition. Specialists can retain narrow
advantages without overturning that supremacy. Earned bond shields, threat
interception and prepared defenses close routine assassination loopholes; his
growth is visible without requiring early dungeon entry or sword mastery.
If unrivaled must also mean unbeatable personal hand-to-hand combat, that
requires a separate choice. The bible does not silently replace a cautious
summoner with a conventional melee hero.

Three battles that test the rules
Early scouting
A level 3 starter has load 1. The protagonist has capacity 3, twelve charge and a
300-metre route. A licensed side corridor ends 220 metres from the exterior
staging point. A question and reply need at least four seconds, so the starter
must notice and evade immediate danger independently.
The team earns twenty fragments, reserves ten, and puts ten toward its next
draw. Recall would cost two charge and require ten safe seconds. Walking out
before pursuit reaches the corridor is wiser than gambling on instant extraction.
The first satisfying victory is judgment made visible.

A broken bridge
A fused specialist has output capacity 8 from predecessors rated 6 and 4, a clear
increase over either alone, and initially retains their combined structural load of
4. With capacity 8, the protagonist can support that specialist, two load 1 scouts
and one load 2 defender.
A new tether-stabilization technique holds a damaged seam while the defender
blocks debris. The scouts guide civilians. Four relays create at least four seconds
of one-way delay. The field leader must decide before instructions return.
Recalling everyone costs sixteen charge. A twelve-charge emergency battery is
insufficient. The team must preserve the bridge or allocate another retreat route;
emotional urgency cannot change the arithmetic.

The final three fronts
At capacity 36, the protagonist coordinates three independently supplied
operator networks. Ten relays make a critical report at least seven seconds late.
One relay's destruction forces rerouting and a visible diversion of engineers.
The ultimate victory first proves direct superiority: his established mass
formation defeats the strongest opposing force. Evacuation and regulator repair
happen at separate sites, showing that overwhelming battle power has
developed alongside breadth. The personal 36-unit budget limits rich individual
channels, not the army he can support through trained operators and charged
infrastructure. Prepared mass deployment, formation buffs and bond defense
consume visible distributed reserves. These abilities are introduced well before
the finale.

Permanent fusion and a new person
What must be true before fusion
Two living, informed adults or species-equivalent adults choose a specific
irreversible transformation. They have compatible architectures established by
non-destructive testing, independent advice and practical alternatives. Proposed
requirements include a stable node, 100 fragments, a full day of reflection after
explanation and separate final consent.
Formation takes thirty uninterrupted minutes. Before the transformation
threshold, interruption cancels safely while consuming preparation energy. After
that threshold, the established automatic process completes. There is no
convenient half-fusion state waiting for a dramatic rescue.
An unconscious, coerced or decision-incapable person cannot consent. A
battlefield threat that punishes refusal is not a shortcut around the premise. At
least one compatible pair should decline without punishment, establishing that a
loving relationship does not require fusion.

What the result is
The result is one body, one voice and one center of agency, with memories of
both prior lives and a personality that blends their histories. It is not two
roommates taking turns at the controls. Memory can be imperfect; emotion and
preference can change. The new person chooses a name and renegotiates
relationships.
A meticulous predecessor and a playful one might become a careful prankster
rather than an average of seriousness and silliness. Remembering affection does
not automatically authorize old intimacy or future service. The protagonist can
feel bittersweet change, pride and joy while welcoming the person now present.
Grief is one possible response, not the mandatory interpretation of fusion.

What power costs
Fusion produces an unmistakably stronger combat form than either predecessor
and unlocks a new integrated signature capability. A proposed output-capacity
guide is the larger of 125% of the stronger predecessor or 80% of their summed
capacity. Inputs of 6 and 4 give a ceiling of 8; inputs of 10 and 1 give 12.5. This is
throughput capacity, not free energy: higher output drains finite charge faster,
creates heat and requires recovery. Ritual reagents supply new structural
capacity. It is a tuning model, not a ceiling on exciting synergy.
Wounds and spent reserves carry forward. Structural link load remains
substantial until later integration improves efficiency. Memories of techniques do
not remove the need to retrain a new body. Further fusion requires new consent,
compatible structure, scarce reagents and enough charge to use its higher
ceiling. It cannot refresh reserves, split for repeated gains or recreate
predecessors. Conservation prevents free resource loops; it does not forbid a
spectacular increase in combat capability.

A fusion that changes the household
Proposed first fusion: Moss, a containment slime, and Vey, a stone
watchkeeper, become Vale around chapters 91–95. If slime becomes the first
summon, decide separately whether the foundational companion should
participate; the default goblin-first version preserves that original friendship
unfused.

Before
Moss enjoys small containers, dislikes dryness and wants to explore beyond
damp passages. Vey feels trapped by an inherited duty to keep watch and wants
a life beyond a single post. Neither is defective or worthless alone. They argue,
cooperate and develop independent friendships long before fusion appears as an
option.
Their compatibility creates a possibility, not an obligation. They explore other
bodies, equipment and living arrangements. Their choice comes from a desired
shared future. The protagonist initially tries to prevent it because he cannot bear
irreversible change; he must recognize that care does not grant ownership of
their lives.

After
Vale has a coherent porous stone body with flexible containment membranes,
one integrated voice and a new sense of humor. Their inherited interest in
vessels becomes a fascination with architecture. They remember the old bowl
and watch stool but ask people to stop setting them out as if two separate
residents will return.
Their first scene includes delight in a new body and an ordinary preference or
boundary. A spectacular tactical demonstration follows after practice. Integration
temporarily disrupts familiar teamwork while making the higher combat ceiling
obvious. The emotional reward and the power reward both deserve room.

The useful new power
Vale can extend controlled semi-rigid shelter through an established tether. This
lets one moving body stabilize a crossing while protecting people underneath.
Neither predecessor could combine those functions in the same location and
body. Vale still cannot heal everyone, regenerate indefinitely or protect an entire
city alone.
The protagonist learns Vale's preferences without demanding performances of
the originals. The household's grief and affection coexist; neither becomes proof
that fusion was simply right or wrong for everyone.

The protagonist and the first companion
The worker
Recommended occupation: a night-shift returns or inventory clerk, with an age in
the early to mid twenties. Exact name, age and country remain open. Work gives
him modest habits of labeling, checking stock and noticing substituted parts. It
does not make him an unexplained tactical genius.
He wants reliable rent, family visits and savings that survive a bad month. His
strength is taking ordinary responsibilities seriously. His flaw is treating
uncertainty as a reason to delay and other people's independence as a safety
failure. This costs opportunities and trust.
He begins physically unfit for combat and remains a commander by choice.
Surface danger, injured friends, public decisions and targeted attacks provide
personal stakes without requiring a dungeon-entry milestone. Fear management
helps him function near gates; it does not need to cure him.
His progression is solitary provider, overprotective handler, accountable leader,
guild founder, and finally a powerful person who can tolerate disagreement. The
temptation at the end is to lock every gate so nobody he loves can leave.

Goblin first
Recommended for immediate dialogue: Nib, a weak field-mender with a broken
tool roll. He wants a workshop no one can confiscate. He asks practical questions,
counts borrowed tools and can repair only what his knowledge and materials
permit. He is neither a hidden deity nor an ideal fusion ingredient.
Nib becomes a field captain through work. He can countermand an unsafe
remote instruction, train apprentices and eventually command a distant front. His
late refusal of fusion strengthens, rather than diminishes, his role.

Slime first
Alternative: a tiny adhesive or filter-feeding slime with readable gestures and
controlled stickiness. It cannot consume anything to steal every skill. This creates
a quieter creature-care beginning; speech develops later, and a goblin can be the
first talking arrival. Both versions must make refusal, comfort and trust legible
from the start.

A small ensemble with independent wants
All names and designs are provisional. Keep six or seven central summons
readable even as the wider community grows.
Moss, containment slime: gentle but possessive about routines and objects.
Wants to explore places inaccessible to solid bodies. Learns that protection can
become confinement. Has independent relationships before any fusion decision.
Vey, stone watchkeeper: formal, dry and durable, with damaged joints and
poor pursuit speed. Wants permission to leave an inherited post. Learns to sleep
while someone else keeps watch. Neither guilt nor fragility exists solely to justify
fusion.
Tess, thread-wisp scout: precise and cutting. Wants to understand privacy
after crowded early housing. Learns that reporting everything is not always
ethical. Later controls access to information without gaining omniscience.
Rook, novice heroic defender: impressive silhouette, inadequate stamina and
a shield he can barely manage. Wants to be useful without performing
inspiration. His first great victory is holding a doorway for seconds. Later he
declines a promotion to remain in rescue work.
Sable, dusk-moth courier: fast-talking, curious and sensitive to being treated
like a drone. Carries finite messages when links fail. Needs rest, makes mistakes
and eventually trains an independent courier organization.
Vale, fused shelter specialist: inherits two histories while building a third life.
Wants to design places people can freely inhabit. They become a leader, not a
permanent symbol of the protagonist's grief.

Relationship movement
Nib teaches Rook practical humility; Rook helps Nib speak publicly. Tess and
Sable argue over privacy and gossip. Moss and Vey disagree about hoarding and
duty before discovering common ambitions. Vale's bond with each teammate
changes separately after fusion.
Give every central summon at least one scene without the protagonist, one
desire unrelated to serving him, one competence he lacks and one consequential

A dungeon economy that pays for growth
Loot is valuable without being infinite
Separate organ cores, charged mineral deposits and infrastructure anchors.
Removing an anchor from a live port resembles damaging a dam, not collecting a
bonus chest. A cleared site's future yield depends on recovery, migration and
remaining material.
As a planning example, a small habitat produces 400 charge units of recoverable
daily surplus after baseline ecological needs. A cautious extraction license allows
200. Mature deposits permit a larger one-time haul, recorded as depletion.
Changing teams at midnight cannot regenerate stock.

interchangeable energy currency. Proposed early capacity is twelve units, with
two units per hour of resting recovery when adequately fed. A link costs 0.1 per
load per hour; a rich feed adds 0.5 per hour. Local relays consume 0.2 per
supported load per hour from finite batteries. Transfers preserve at most 80% of
input charge. These are fictional tuning units, not joules.

taxes. A proposed 15% reserve leaves 1,190 before owner withdrawals and
recruitment investment. Credits and tax treatment are fictional. One 900-credit

Foreshadowing that pays off
Keep early clues true
The circled exit, episode 1: an anxious habit becomes a practical shared
evacuation symbol by the siege. Do not reveal that the circle was an ancient
royal crest.
The cheap bowls, episodes 2–8: hospitality becomes crowding, then a
communal kitchen. At chapter 200 the original bowl survives as an object people
actually use.
The harness ownership question, episode 4: equipment becomes pay,
property and departure rights. A recruit later leaves with their possessions and
remains a friend.
The wrong map, episodes 5–6: first an operational hazard, then repeated
deliberate alterations, finally evidence locating the Registrar's limited authority
nodes. The early wall was real; the later reveal explains its cause.
The abandoned bag, episode 7: a small sacrifice becomes an established
reserve policy and a major choice to preserve people over an exceptional cache
during the siege. Not every later choice must repeat the same answer
mechanically.
The first field decision, episodes 9–10: a captain's autonomy grows through
disagreements and training. By the finale, a distant front wins before the
protagonist can authorize its decisive move.

Character clues
Moss's containers and Vey's watch duty become Vale's interest in architecture
and capacity to rest. These are blended influences, not proof that two intact
personalities remain inside.
Tess's habit of overreporting leads to a privacy disagreement, then access
controls that prevent a single compromised feed from exposing the whole
network. Rook's heavy shield becomes a trained signature defense, not a sudden
effortless prop.

Tone pacing and visual storytelling
Keep the main pleasure visible
In an average twenty-chapter arc, roughly ten to twelve chapters center on
exploration, preparation directly tied to a hunt, combat and boss resolution.
Three or four develop recruits, training and loot; three or four develop
relationships and recovery; one or two handle institutional consequences. These
are flexible targets, not a panel quota.
Slow progression should contain frequent small victories. A repaired glove, a
precise new skill, a successful hunt or a rare draw can feel rewarding before the
team becomes powerful. Do not repeatedly consume every gain with a new bill.

Comedy and horror
Comedy comes from incompatible habits, cramped housing, literal
interpretations and pride. The goblin correcting a badly assembled shelf can be
funny without making it stupid. The protagonist's careful budget can collide with
a recruit's expensive taste without mocking poverty.
Horror comes from scale, silence, ecological wrongness and incomplete
information: a familiar room without insects, a feed that returns an impossible
pressure reading, a guardian defending something the hunters cannot yet see.
Avoid constant gore as a substitute for suspense. Do not place a punchline inside
the immediate emotional aftermath of a permanent loss or fusion decision.

The selected visual direction
Use the selected blend: expressive faces and comic timing associated with The
Greatest Estate Developer, alongside clean proportions and dramatic action
lighting associated with Omniscient Reader. Translate those qualities into original
designs rather than copying characters, costumes or signature compositions.
Give every central character a clear silhouette and readable expression system
at phone size. Preserve scale, gear, injuries and handedness. Establish tactical
geography before action accelerates.
Use vertical space for waiting, distance and reveal. Show communication delay
through interrupted rhythm and viewpoint cuts, not repeated explanatory text.
Differentiate interface text from dialogue, but keep UI sparse and decision-
relevant.
Fusion should create one coherent new design with recognizable influences.
Avoid a simple half-and-half costume or two dialogue fonts implying separate
minds. The broad blend is chosen; original palettes, line treatment and character
sheets still require development.

Exploit tests before a scene is locked
Recruitment and growth
Can money buy infinite draws? No. Bound fragments require a finite
attributed contribution to a real stabilization event. Money buys support, not
identities.
Can spectators multiply rewards? No. The event pool is shared. Late higher-
tier yields grow because the team stabilizes larger gradients, not because more
people look at one core.
Can failed transfers reroll? No. Selection and reservation survive interruption
or refund state. Candidate withdrawal remains protected without becoming a
shopping exploit.
Can harmless farming create infinite levels? No. Adaptation requires
genuine learning and consolidation. Routine work can earn income without
endlessly raising combat ceilings.

Bodies fusion and transport
Can fusion create a power loop? No. It increases usable channel capacity and
synergy while consuming finite reagents and charge. Higher output drains more
energy. There is no free split, copied predecessor or refreshed reserve.
Can recall erase every threat? No. Formation needs time, charge, a valid
route and protection. Cargo limits apply before transfer starts.
Can a huge army march without links? Yes. Ordinary travel is a legitimate
strategy. Such troops do not receive free high-fidelity feeds or recall. Late
prepared command infrastructure can support mass deployment without
personal micromanagement.
Can the commander be killed by any stray bullet forever? No. Earned
bond defenses, guards and prepared shelters close routine assassination routes.
Exceptional attacks still require established counterplay and costs; invulnerability
is not assumed.

World and market
Can clearing regenerate loot? No. Reproduction, migration, workshops and
geological inflow have material costs and rates.
Can energy conversion create profit from nothing? No. Every round trip
loses usable charge. Scarcity, portability and special structure can still make a
product valuable.
Can victory grant a nation? No. Conquest and defense establish leverage;
territories, residents and recognized agreements establish a polity. Show both
when the empire begins.

Continuity and chapter production checks
Keep one authoritative ledger
For each expedition, record physically present characters, active high-fidelity
links, route and relay path, latency, charge stores, cooldowns, injuries, food and
equipment, fragment balances, pity counters, map knowledge, field authority and
the actual exit plan.
For each named person, record goals, relationships, voice, current abilities,
limits, possessions and unresolved promises. After fusion, create one new record
with inherited histories and current preferences. Do not silently keep writing the
predecessors as separate speakers.
For each habitat, record energy and food sources, water, inhabitants, claims,
pressure trend, previous damage, extraction and waste. A return visit should
reflect what happened before.

Check a chapter as a comic
Does the opening establish location and immediate desire? Can the reader tell
who acts, what changes and why? Is the decisive ability already demonstrated?
Does the opponent act competently with available information? Is there a
completed small payoff, not only another postponed answer?
Thumbnail in the intended vertical format before polishing dialogue. Review text
density, silhouette readability, spatial continuity and the timing of a reveal. A
good prose synopsis does not prove that a scroll sequence works.

Protect progression
Track higher-tier fragment yields explicitly. The early ten-net-fragments model
must not require hundreds of fully depicted outings later. Mastered routes can be
montaged; higher-grade stabilization provides larger finite pools; routine staff
work cannot create passive infinite attribution.
A proposed mid-tier net contribution of 50–100 fragments per substantial
operation and late multi-team contribution of 200–500 can support the planned
recruitment curve. These values require event-level accounting and remain
tuning proposals.
Before publication, run three paper simulations: ten early outings, the first fusion
hunt and the final siege. Reconcile charge, travel, injury and rewards. If a clever
legal tactic works, let intelligent characters use it rather than inventing a
retroactive prohibition.

The few choices that materially affect scripts
1. First companion: goblin for immediate dialogue and negotiation, or slime for
a quieter creature-care opening.
2. Setting and identity: a fictional country or a specifically researched real
setting; then choose names, occupation and family details consistently.
3. First personal entry: recommended late, brief and earned if wanted, around
chapters 130–150; fully remote remains viable. Neither choice requires becoming
a melee protagonist.
4. Fusion emphasis: joyful new possibility with bittersweet change is the
default. The degree of grief, ceremony and identity debate can be adjusted
without changing permanence or the stronger new form.
5. Empire form and timing: a sovereign inter-gate empire is the eventual
destination. Decide whether its mature expansion follows chapter 200, and
whether its constitution is monarchical or federated.
6. Design and romance: develop original designs within the selected visual
blend. Add romance only if desired; the main emotional engine already rests on
family and companion bonds.
Exact percentages, terminology and chapter boundaries can be tuned after the
first scripts are tested. These six choices matter more than approving dozens of
small numbers in advance.
